# Paweł Pawlikowski

> **Why relevant:** Master of visual restraint and formal precision — black-and-white, academy ratio, deep European sensibility. As a VFX Supervisor, working with directors like Pawlikowski means serving the image, not dominating it. His films show what's possible with rigorous visual language and minimal spectacle.

## Visual Signature

- **Black and white.** Both *Ida* and *Cold War* — a deliberate, non-nostalgic choice to evoke the texture of Polish cinema from the era depicted.
- **Academy ratio (1.33:1 / 1.37:1).** Forces the frame into something more like a painting or portrait. Pawlikowski said the unusual compositions were designed to "make the audience feel uncertain, to watch in a different way."
- **Negative space in the frame.** Figures placed at the bottom or edges of the frame — sky and emptiness carry as much weight as the subjects.
- **Stillness.** Very little camera movement. He works more like a photographer arranging a composition than a director covering action.
- **Economy.** *Cold War* is 88 minutes. *Ida* is 80 minutes. Nothing is wasted.

## Production Trivia — *Ida* (2013)

Łukasz Żal stepped in as co-DP after Ryszard Lenczewski fell ill after just 8 days of shooting — it was effectively Żal's first feature. The black-and-white image was monitored on set using a real-time B&W LUT applied to HD reference dailies, so the crew could see how colours would translate before committing. The production was interrupted mid-shoot by an early snowstorm; Pawlikowski used the two-week hiatus to rewrite, find new locations, and rehearse — and credits that break with making the film cohere. Producers reportedly told him: *"Paul, you are no longer a student, don't be silly"* when he proposed filming in black-and-white and 4:3.

---

## Production Trivia — *Cold War* (2018)

Łukasz Żal returned, this time shooting in 1.37:1 — the old Academy ratio. The film is semi-autobiographical: the two lead characters are based on Pawlikowski's own parents. Principal locations spanned Poland, Paris, Yugoslavia, and Berlin. Editor Jarosław Kamiński shaped the film's remarkable compression — entire years of a love affair elided in a single cut. Cannes 2018, Best Director.

## Personal Life

Pawlikowski's wife was diagnosed with terminal cancer in 2006. He paused his career to care for her and their two children; she died months later. He spent five years away from filmmaking, teaching at the National Film and Television School (Beaconsfield, UK). This hiatus shaped the intimate, grief-adjacent quality of *Ida* and *Cold War* — both films about loss, displacement, and impossible love.
