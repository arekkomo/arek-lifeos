### **Key Takeaways**

- Process Mapping involves creating a visual diagram to outline a series of steps and decisions in a process, making intangible workflows visible.  

- The four basic symbols used in Process Mapping are squares (steps), diamonds (decisions), rounded rectangles (start/stop), and arrows (connections).  

- When creating a Process Map, ensure each step includes a verb-noun pairing (e.g., "write script") to clearly define actions.  

- Avoid duplicating steps unnecessarily in your Process Map, as it can create confusion and misrepresent the process.  

- Process Mapping is valuable for identifying inefficiencies, miscommunications, delays, and redundancies in business processes.  

- It can be done with simple tools like sticky notes, whiteboards, or digital whiteboard tools such as Canva or Winsicle.  

- Process Mapping is useful in two scenarios: improving an existing process or designing a new one.  

- Defining the start and stop points (the scope) of the process is critical to creating a focused and effective Process Map.  

- Depending on your goals (e.g., improving customer experience, reducing miscommunication, or increasing profitability), you can enhance the Process Map by adding elements like customer journey details, work streams, or time costs.  

- Process Mapping is especially effective when done collaboratively with a team, as it fosters shared understanding and uncovers hidden insights.
