- **Cinematic Prompts** (0:30): These prompts focus on controlling the camera work and movement within a scene. They describe *how* the scene is observed, not just *what* is being seen. Examples include static shots, zooming, rotating, panning, shaky handheld cameras, and bird's eye views. These can also be combined to create more complex camera movements.
- **Timestamp Prompts** (2:26): This method allows you to divide an AI video into multiple segments and specify different camera movements or actions for each segment. This provides more granular control over the sequence of events within the video.
- **Cutscene Prompts** (3:17): These prompts enable the generation of multiple camera angles within a single AI video, allowing for cuts to different perspectives to tell a full story. This can turn the AI video generator into a storyboard tool or video editor. A caution is noted that if the cuts are too different from the original video, consistency can be hard to maintain (4:32).
- **GPT Prompts** (5:16): This involves using an AI language model, like ChatGPT, to help write prompts. By providing the AI with documentation on how to prompt for specific AI video models, it can generate detailed prompts including elements like cinematography, sound effects, style, and lighting. However, a limitation is that AI documentation often doesn't specify what the AI video generator is *bad* at, leading to potential issues (8:00).
- **Anchor Prompts** (10:11): These prompts are used to remind the AI of important elements in the scene that it might otherwise ignore or misinterpret. By "anchoring" specific details in the prompt (e.g., physical appearance, relationships between subjects, or unseen parts of a character), you can help the AI maintain consistency and accuracy in the generated video.
- **Image Prompts** (13:04): This technique involves providing the AI video generator with a reference image to create more consistent and visually stunning shots. It's particularly useful for generating surrealistic scenes or maintaining a consistent character across multiple shots and camera angles. This also includes "start and end frame prompting" (15:58), where images define the beginning and end of a video sequence.
- **Negative Prompts** (16:18): Instead of telling the AI what you want, negative prompts specify what you *don't* want to see in the video. This can be an easier way to remove unwanted elements or behaviors from the generated output.
