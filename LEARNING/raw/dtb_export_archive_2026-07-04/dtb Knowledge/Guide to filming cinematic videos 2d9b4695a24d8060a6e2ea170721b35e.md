# Guide to filming cinematic videos

This video provides a comprehensive guide to filming cinematic videos, covering various stages from **story conceptualization** (0:24) to **post-production and workflow tips** (4:46).

Here's a breakdown of the key areas discussed:

- **Storytelling Fundamentals** (0:24): The video emphasizes that a good story needs **structure**, a **character/subject**, and **development**. The creator suggests focusing on stories that genuinely interest you, even if the process of writing can be challenging and iterative.
- **Shot List and Sequence** (1:54): The goal of a shot list is to match visuals with the story by mixing camera angles, typically starting wide and moving tight. The video illustrates this with an example of shooting a scene, showing how to use establishing shots, close-ups for emotion, and action shots to move the narrative forward.
- **Lighting Techniques** (3:00): Lighting is highlighted as a crucial element for cinematic shots. The creator demonstrates using **haze to diffuse light**, setting **background practical lights** to establish light source, and implementing the "**cinematic triangle**" with a key light to create contrast and depth. The importance of separating depth lighting (practical lights) from subject lighting (key light) is also discussed.
- **Editing Process** (4:46): Editing is where the video comes to life through **music, color, and rhythm**. The first step is to choose music that dictates the pace and emotion, then edit shots to its rhythm. The video also mentions the importance of narration and offers a free audio preset for voiceovers.
- **Color Grading** (5:33): Color grading is presented as the "life source" of a video, transforming lifeless footage. The creator explains their process, which involves ensuring proper lighting, ISO, and picture profile during shooting (e.g., low ISO, picture profile 8, cinematic triangle). They then use a one-click color grading preset in DaVinci Resolve that mimics film depth, though minor adjustments to white balance and exposure may sometimes be needed.
- **Thumbnail Creation** (6:34): The creator reveals their method for creating thumbnails by recording a scene and pulling stills from it. Key advice includes using **contrasting colors** (green, blue, red) and good lighting.
- **Behind-the-Scenes (BTS) Tips** (7:39): This section offers practical advice, including using a **slider for panning shots** (7:02) and a **sunset lamp for warm light** (7:49). The creator emphasizes that filmmaking is often a process of trial and error, encouraging adaptation and learning from failures.
- **Pre-Production with Milanote** (9:46): The video showcases how the creator uses Milanote to plan and organize their video projects. This includes finding contrasting colors for the video's aesthetic, using checklists to stay on track, relying on visual inspiration by dragging and dropping photos to create mood boards and connect to shot lists, and curating color grade ideas. A key feature highlighted is the ability to **add music to the board** to help write scripts that are on tempo with the chosen song.
- **Embracing Failure** (13:09): The creator concludes by encouraging viewers to **fail often and fail big** in their creative journeys. They explain that perfectionism can hinder learning, and it's through failure that one truly learns and improves.
