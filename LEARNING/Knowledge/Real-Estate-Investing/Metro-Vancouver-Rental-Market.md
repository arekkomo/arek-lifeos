# Metro Vancouver Rental Market (July 2026)

## Current State
Rental market has been surprisingly resilient despite everything else going wrong in the Vancouver investment condo space. Vacancy rates ticked up to about 3-4% downtown as smaller investor-flipped units back to landlords rather than selling at a loss, but actual rents haven't cratered because tenant affordability keeps getting worse and tenants have very limited options.

## Average Rents (one-bedroom)
| Location | Rent Range/Month |
|---|---|
| Downtown Vancouver | $1,850–$2,100 |
| Eastern Suburbs (East Van, Burnaby, Coquitlam, New West) | $1,600–$1,800 |

## Supply Pressure Points
- Government buyout program adds ~2,200 subsidized rental units to Metro Vancouver stock by 2027 — puts downward pressure on rents where they land
- "Try before you buy" developer programs emerged April 2026 as alternative way developers tried to move inventory without direct discounting
- Developer fire-sale pricing on secondary-market inventory (sounds like bargain but faces 18 months of government-subsidized tenant competition)

## Yield Dynamics
Budget-tier prices fell more sharply and quickly than rents did — yields actually **improved by ~two percentage points** at the absolute bottom of market since early 2025. The math gets worse as you move up from $600K tier because higher-end units have higher strata fees and proportionally smaller rent premiums relative to purchase price.
