# Directing Craft Reference Sources

This source ledger supports [[Script-to-Scene-Analysis]], [[Purposeful-Shot-Lists]], and [[AI-Video-Scene-Packet]]. It privileges durable professional craft texts over universal prescriptions: their frameworks are tested vocabulary for making and communicating choices, not formulas that replace taste.

## Sources

## 1 — Rabiger

**Michael Rabiger & Mick Hurbis-Cherrier, _Directing: Film Techniques and Aesthetics_, 6th ed.** — a broad directing workflow spanning script analysis, staging, shot design and collaboration. [Publisher / catalog](https://www.routledge.com/Directing-Film-Techniques-and-Aesthetics/Rabiger-Hurbis-Cherrier/p/book/9780367248489)

## 2 — Weston

**Judith Weston, _Directing Actors: Creating Memorable Performances for Film and Television_, 25th Anniversary ed.** — actionable language for playable objectives, obstacles, actions, beats and actor-direction. [Publisher](https://mwp.com/product/directing-actors/)

## 3 — Katz

**Steven D. Katz, _Film Directing: Cinematic Motion_, 2nd ed.** — planning images, staging and camera movement as cinematic storytelling. [Publisher](https://mwp.com/product/film-directing-cinematic-motion/)

## 4 — Brown

**Blain Brown, _Cinematography: Theory and Practice: Image Making for Cinematographers and Directors_, 4th ed.** — visual grammar, lenses, composition, continuity and the director–cinematographer decision space. [Publisher / catalog](https://www.routledge.com/Cinematography-Theory-and-Practice-Image-Making-for-Cinematographers-and-Directors/Brown/p/book/9780367229266)

## 5 — Murch

**Walter Murch, _In the Blink of an Eye_, 2nd ed.** — editorial priorities and the relationship among emotion, story, rhythm, eye trace, screen plane and spatial continuity. [Publisher](https://www.silmanjamespress.com/shop/film/in-the-blink-of-an-eye/)

## Scope and confidence

The pages citing this ledger turn the sources into a compact working method. Terms such as objective, obstacle, beat, turn, staging, coverage, eyeline, screen direction and continuity are broadly shared production vocabulary. Exact framing, lens, performance direction, coverage quantity, camera movement and cut point remain context-dependent directorial choices.
