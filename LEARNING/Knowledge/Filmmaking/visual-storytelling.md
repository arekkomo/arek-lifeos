# Visual Storytelling

The craft of using image, motion, sound, and structure to communicate narratively. In the short-form content era, attention engineering has become a core skill alongside traditional narrative craft.

## The Visual Stun Gun (first 1-2 seconds)

Short-form content lives and dies in the first 1-2 seconds. The principle: **force a pattern interrupt** before the viewer scrolls.

Three levers:
1. **Motion** — sudden or unexpected movement that the eye can't ignore
2. **Color** — high contrast, unusual palette, or saturation spike
3. **Brightness** — flash cut or strong light that registers before cognitive processing

This is the "visual stun gun" — a visual event that arrests scrolling, not by communicating content but by triggering a low-level visual reflex.

## Short-form narrative structure

After the hook, structure matters:
- **Hook** → **Promise** (what will you learn/see/feel?) → **Payoff** → **Residue** (what do they take away?)
- Compress the promise: fewer seconds to establish stakes
- Avoid slow burns unless the hook bought enough attention

## Storyboarding for AI-assisted production

The Nano Banana Storyboard format (from Notion notes) documents per-shot:
- Lighting specification
- Color grade target
- Sound/atmosphere notes
- Focus target

This structured approach enables AI-assisted production because each shot has explicit parameters that can be passed to generation tools.

## Relationship to AI filmmaking

A disciplined storyboard is a natural bridge between human creative direction and AI generation:
- Shot specs → prompt inputs for [[runway-ml|Runway]], [[minimax|MiniMax]], [[kling-ai|Kling AI]]
- Lighting/grade specs → DaVinci Resolve color targets
- Pre-defined look enables consistency across AI-generated and live footage
