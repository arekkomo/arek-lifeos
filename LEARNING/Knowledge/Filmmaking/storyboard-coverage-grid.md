# Storyboard Coverage Grid

Use this grid when a single story beat needs a fast, coherent visual exploration. It supplies coverage variety without changing the story world or character identity.

> **Source:** [[Storytelling Prompts — Notion Source Capture]]

## 3×3 camera grammar

| Purpose | Shot | What it must reveal |
|---|---|---|
| Context | Extreme long shot | The environment, scale, isolation, or approach path. |
| Context | Long shot | The full body/object and its relationship to the space. |
| Context | Medium long / American | Stance, costume, body language, and usable geography. |
| Performance | Medium | The central action or decision. |
| Performance | Medium close-up | Expression, tension, or a micro-interaction. |
| Performance | Close-up | Emotional clarity or a consequential object-facing moment. |
| Detail | Extreme close-up | A symbolic, tactile, or causal detail: eye, hand, device, weapon, texture. |
| Perspective | Low angle | Power, threat, aspiration, or scale from below. |
| Perspective | High angle | Spatial clarity, fragility, surveillance, or tactical overview. |

## Coverage discipline

- **Hold continuity constant:** subject identity, costume, key prop, environmental state, lighting direction, and grade.
- **Vary one visual question per panel:** scale, geography, performance, detail, or power relation.
- **Protect editability:** frame the action so a cut between panels would make spatial and emotional sense.
- **Select deliberately:** mark a panel as the hero still only after checking identity, lens feel, directional light, and the available negative space for motion.

## AI-generation note

A grid prompt can give image models a shot plan, but it cannot guarantee exact multi-panel continuity. If exact identity and production continuity matter, generate the selected hero panel separately with the board’s continuity ledger and use it as the downstream I2V reference.
