# Purposeful Shot Lists

Build the shot list from the completed [[Script-to-Scene-Analysis]] and staging pass. Each shot must have a job: orient, reveal, restrict, align the viewer, register a reaction, carry an action across a cut, or make the turn legible. This planning relationship is consistent with [[Directing Craft Reference Sources#1 — Rabiger|[1]]], [[Directing Craft Reference Sources#3 — Katz|[3]]] and [[Directing Craft Reference Sources#4 — Brown|[4]]].

## Coverage design pass

1. **Anchor geography.** Choose the shot(s) that establish the playable space, axes, entrances, exits and actor paths.
2. **Protect performance.** Decide which beats need sustained two-person dynamics, a reaction, a detail/insert, or an independent single.
3. **Plan options at the turn.** Cover the changed relationship, not simply the dialogue again.
4. **Check editorial handoffs.** Give each intended cut a reason: new information, response, change in attention, completed action, or controlled contrast.
5. **Remove redundant coverage.** If two shots tell the same story with no distinct editorial option, omit one.

The objective is not “get every size.” It is sufficient, intentional material for the edit; Murch’s hierarchy gives emotion and story priority over purely spatial correctness when those values compete. [[Directing Craft Reference Sources#5 — Murch|[5]]]

## Shot-list template

| # | Beat / job | Frame + subject | Blocking / eyeline | Camera | Sound / edit intention | AI production note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Orient / establish pressure | WS two-shot; door and table visible | Both faces playable; preserve 180° axis | Locked | Hold long enough to teach geography | Make this the spatial reference frame. |
| 02 | Align with A’s tactic | MCU A; negative space toward B | A steps into B’s space | Slow push only if the pressure escalates | Cut on B’s refusal or A’s decision | Lock character, wardrobe, set and screen direction. |
| 03 | Turn / reaction | CU B | B stops moving; eyeline retains A | Static or reframe after the turn | Hold past dialogue if reaction is the event | Generate as a separate reaction plate; preserve lighting and eyeline. |
| 04 | New condition | Wider two-shot or altered single | New distance / power relation visible | Pull back, track, or static—choose one motivated grammar | Cut/hold according to aftermath | Use the post-turn block as a new continuity state. |

Replace the illustrative rows with only the shots the scene needs.

## Decision rules

- **Framing:** select the distance that gives the audience the needed access to environment, action or interior response; it is not a fixed emotion dictionary. [[Directing Craft Reference Sources#4 — Brown|[4]]]
- **Blocking:** alter distance, level, orientation and access to make changing relationships playable and visible. Preserve eyelines, props and actor positions across coverage. [[Directing Craft Reference Sources#1 — Rabiger|[1]]]
- **Movement:** move the camera when a change in attention, knowledge, proximity or energy earns it; a move without a story job is decoration. [[Directing Craft Reference Sources#3 — Katz|[3]]] See also [[Module-01-Visual-Language]].
- **Editing and pacing:** cut where the viewer’s needed attention changes; leave a hold when the reaction, discomfort or duration is the point. Emotion and story outrank rhythm, then eye trace, screen plane and spatial continuity in Murch’s stated hierarchy. [[Directing Craft Reference Sources#5 — Murch|[5]]]

## Common failure modes

- **Shot list before analysis:** technically varied coverage with no clear dramatic centre.
- **Coverage by shot-size checklist:** every line receives the same master/medium/close-up pattern, flattening beats and wasting generation budget.
- **Unmotivated movement:** visual activity calls attention to itself instead of the change in the scene.
- **Broken screen direction or eyelines:** reverses the viewer’s spatial model unless deliberately used as disruption.
- **Reaction omitted:** the plot information is covered but its human consequence is not.
- **No edit logic:** shots describe camera hardware but not when/why the edit changes.
- **AI continuity drift:** independently strong clips disagree on actor position, gaze, prop state, lighting, lens language or direction of travel.

## Principle versus choice

Preserve readable geography and generate coverage that can carry essential beats—these are robust production principles. The number of shots, use of a master, exact camera side, lens, duration and movement are creative choices governed by POV, performance, genre, resources and intended edit.
