# One More: caption guidelines

## The job of a caption

A caption should give the image a reason to stop, save, reply, or visit the profile. It is not a press release and should never repeat what the viewer can already see. Write for a guest deciding: **what is this, how will it feel, and what can I do next?** Instagram remains a frequent brand-contact channel: Sprout reports 60% of consumers interact with brand content there at least multiple times a week. [Sprout Social, 2025/26](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/instagram-statistics/)

## Voice framework: “the table is being held for you”

| Trait | Do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Warm | sensory, human, specific: “butter still sizzling at the edge” | generic luxury: “an elevated culinary experience” |
| Inviting | write as a host: “come hungry”, “save this for your next coast day” | hard sell: “book now!!!” |
| Local | name the ingredient, maker, season or place when true | vague “locally sourced” claims |
| Confident | short declarative sentences; let the dish lead | apologetic or over-explained copy |
| Honest | say “testing”, “first table”, “coming soon” when applicable | invented scarcity or unconfirmed opening details |

## Caption architecture

Use this four-part sequence. **70–150 words** is the default; use 25–50 words for a visual hero, 150–250 only when the story genuinely earns it. The “sweet spot” is operational, not a platform law: test short versus medium captions against saves, shares, profile visits and DMs.

1. **Hook (first 125 characters):** a concrete sensory moment, a tension, or a location promise.
2. **Payoff (1–3 short lines):** what happened; name the dish/ingredient/place.
3. **Meaning (1–2 lines):** why it matters to a guest — a ritual, season, person or small detail.
4. **One CTA:** invite a useful low-friction response.

### Templates

**Dish Reel**
> The butter hits the pan before the coast wakes up.\n\n[Dish] with [specific ingredient], made for slow tables and a second glass of [drink].\n\nWould you start with this or save room for dessert?

**Place + food carousel**
> A long lunch is better when the road there looks like this.\n\nSwipe from [place/arrival] to [dish]. This is the kind of day we are building on the Sunshine Coast.\n\nSave it for the next coastal escape.

**Founder/host post**
> Meet the hands behind tonight’s [dish].\n\n[One true, specific sentence about choice or process.]\n\nWhat would you want on the first One More table?

## CTA bank — one per caption

- “Which slide would you order first?”
- “Save this for your next Sunshine Coast day.”
- “Send this to the person who always orders one more plate.”
- “Tell us: window seat or kitchen counter?”
- “Want the first menu note? Follow along.”
- “Know a local maker we should meet? Tag them below.”

Do **not** combine multiple asks, demand tags/follows, or use comment/like/follow exchange language. Authentic comments and replies are valuable community signals; artificial engagement schemes are addressed in [[Growth-Tactics-New-Accounts]].

## Pre-publish checklist

- First line works without “see more.”
- Every factual claim (availability, location, partner, opening date) is verified.
- One CTA only; no “link in bio” unless the linked destination is live and relevant.
- Add alt text that describes the food and setting, not keyword stuffing. Meta added API support for image-post alt text in 2025, although not for Reels/Stories through that field. [Meta, Content Publishing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/)
- Add the selected 5–8 tags from [[Posting-Strategy]] and tag only real collaborators, makers or locations.
