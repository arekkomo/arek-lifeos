# Storytelling Prompts — Source Capture

## What the source provides

The page is a public prompt guide intended to be used with an initial **base/reference image**. Its central instruction is a *story-to-storyboard meta-prompt*: interpret a short story synopsis, preserve the supplied reference-image details, and write — rather than generate — a detailed image prompt for a 3×3 cinematic storyboard.

## Source workflow

1. Identify the subject(s), defining appearance, environment/tone, narrative beat, and implied lighting/mood from a synopsis.
2. Retain the source-image identity cues and story-specific details.
3. Produce nine shots of the same subject(s), environment, wardrobe, lighting, and atmosphere.
4. Cover the story beat through a fixed progression of shot sizes and angles.
5. Leave a technical retrieval marker (`Extract the still x.y`) so one board panel can be selected for a subsequent still-generation pass.

## Nine-panel coverage specified

| Row | Panel | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| 1: context | 1 | Extreme long shot — world and scale |
| 1: context | 2 | Long shot — full subject in environment |
| 1: context | 3 | Medium long / American shot — stance and emotion |
| 2: core coverage | 4 | Medium shot — action or attitude |
| 2: core coverage | 5 | Medium close-up — expression/tension |
| 2: core coverage | 6 | Close-up — emotional clarity |
| 3: details & angles | 7 | Extreme close-up — symbolic detail, hand, eye, prop, or texture |
| 3: details & angles | 8 | Low angle — dramatic, heroic, or imposing treatment |
| 3: details & angles | 9 | High angle — spatial overview, vulnerability, or action clarity |

## Explicit continuity requirements

- Same subject(s), clothing, hairstyle, accessories, props, and weapons in all nine panels.
- Same lighting conditions, color grade, environment, and weather.
- Photoreal textures, shot-appropriate cinematic depth of field, and credible focal-length/camera behavior.
- Each frame must be a distinct shot, not merely a crop of one composition.

## Practical interpretation

This is a **previsualization and still-selection method**, not a video-generation prompt format. The selected still should become the reference/keyframe for a separate single-shot video prompt; that video prompt must still describe one continuous action and camera move.
