### **Key Takeaways**

Anamorphic lenses create a widescreen aspect ratio (e.g., 2.35:1 or 2.39:1), while spherical lenses generally have more square aspect ratios (e.g., 1.33:1 or 1.85:1).  

Spherical lenses produce sharper images with minimal distortion, while anamorphic lenses introduce more distortion, falloff, and dramatic lens flares.  

Bokeh differs between the two lens types: spherical lenses create circular bokeh, while anamorphic lenses create oval-shaped bokeh.  

Anamorphic lenses require more light due to their higher T-stop, increasing lighting costs and production design requirements.  

Spherical lenses offer more focal length options, whereas anamorphic lens sets typically have fewer choices.  

Filmmakers use lens choices to influence storytelling and audience perception, as seen in *There Will Be Blood* (widescreen for vast landscapes) and *The Lighthouse* (square format for isolation).  

Lens characteristics affect the psychological feel of a film, such as the sharp, immersive look of *The Tree of Life* versus the soft, nostalgic imagery of *Moonlight*.  

Practical considerations, such as budget and lighting constraints, play a crucial role in determining whether anamorphic or spherical lenses are used.
