### Key takeaways

- Get **morning sunlight exposure** (within ~10–15 minutes of waking, without sunglasses) to reset your circadian rhythm and boost alertness.
- Delay caffeine for about **90–120 minutes** after waking to allow natural cortisol to decline and allow the brain to “wake up” more naturally.
- Do a **hard cognitive task** early (roughly 90 minutes of focus) — engage the mind when it’s fresh to maximise productivity.
- Exercise soon after — whether strength, cardio or both — to raise heart rate and support metabolism.
- Use **cold exposure** (e.g., cold shower or ice bath) for benefits in recovery, mood, resilience.
- Stay well-hydrated and consider electrolyte replenishment early in the day to support performance.
- Keep your sleep schedule consistent. Waking early works only if sleep quality and duration are solid.

---

### Why it matters

- Morning light influences melatonin/cortisol cycles, which impact energy, mood, productivity.
- Delaying caffeine avoids the “artificial boost” trap and helps your system rely on endogenous rhythms.
- Early deep work leverages the brain’s fresh capacity rather than reactive modes (email, social media).
- Physical movement early builds momentum and sets a tone of action for the day.
- Cold exposure triggers physiologic stress adaptation—positive for resilience, mood, alertness.
- Hydration + electrolytes support cellular function, especially if you’ve been fasting or sleeping for ~7–9 hours.
- Consistency builds sustainable benefits rather than sporadic “hacks”.

### How you might apply it (modified for you)

- Right after waking in Vancouver: go outside in the daylight (even if overcast) for 10 minutes before grabbing your phone.
- Hold off on coffee for ~90 minutes; maybe use that time for prepping your filming gear, planning drone shots or sketching storyboards (since you’re a VFX/content creator).
- Then hit a 30-45 minute workout (could be mobility + cardio) to get your body and mind aligned for shooting or editing.
- After workout, you could take a cold shower or contrast shower (warm → cold) to sharpen up before diving into editing or AI-generation work.
- Have a hydrating drink early (water + pinch of mineral salt) and ensure you’ve slept 7-9 hours regularly.
- Use this sequence consistently so your brain/body expect it—it becomes your “productive launch pad”.
