Here are the books mentioned in the video with short descriptions:

- Grit by Angela Duckworth: This book argues that passion and perseverance are more important for success than talent.
- Thinking in Bets by Annie Duke: This book teaches that you should evaluate decisions based on the process used to make them, not on the outcome, and that you should think in terms of probabilities rather than certainties.
- Flow by Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi: This book describes the mental state of being so absorbed in a task that you lose your sense of time and self.
- The War of Art by Steven Pressfield: This book identifies "the resistance," a force that prevents people from doing creative work, and encourages acting like a professional to overcome it.
- The Stoic Challenge by William Irvine: This book teaches you to reframe adversity and setbacks as opportunities for growth and resilience.
- Give and Take by Adam Grant: This book categorizes people into givers, takers, and matchers, and explains how givers can be the most successful without being taken advantage of.
- Influence by Robert Cialdini: This classic outlines six principles of persuasion: reciprocity, scarcity, authority, consistency, liking, and social proof.
- The Righteous Mind by Jonathan Haidt: This book explores how people's moral decisions are often based on emotional reactions rather than rational thought.
- Mindset by Carol Dweck: This book distinguishes between a fixed mindset (abilities are finite) and a growth mindset (abilities can be developed), and advocates for the latter.
- Moneyball by Michael Lewis: Although about baseball, this book provides lessons on challenging industry norms and thinking differently.
- Man's Search for Meaning by Viktor Frankl: Written by a Holocaust survivor, this book argues that the primary human drive is the search for meaning, even in the most brutal conditions.
