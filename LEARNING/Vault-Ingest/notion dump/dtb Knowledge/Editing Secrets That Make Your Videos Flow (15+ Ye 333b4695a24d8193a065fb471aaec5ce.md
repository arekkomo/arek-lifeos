## 💡 Key Insights

- Edit audio first before any visuals - this single change reveals timing issues and inspires new shot ideas before you've even filmed
- Pacing variation is what keeps viewers engaged: strategically alternate between high-energy fast cuts and slower emotional moments
- Your soundtrack, shot length, and camera movement are three levers that all control pacing and must work together

## 📋 All Points & Tips

- Start your edits with audio only before touching any video clips - lay down voiceover, music, and sound effects first
- Record your hook and intro voiceover before you even start shooting to identify pacing issues early
- Finding music that matches your intended energy will reveal where voiceover is too long or pacing needs adjustment
- Starting with audio gives you new inspiration for shots you can still add to your shot list before filming
- Shot length affects pacing: short snappy cuts create tension while longer shots convey emotion or calmness
- Shot motion shapes energy: chaotic handheld adds energy while smooth stable frames calm things down
- Music and sound set the heartbeat of your story - driving beats push forward, softer music lets it breathe
- Silence can be a powerful storytelling tool when used intentionally
- Keeping the pace high the entire time overwhelms viewers; keeping it slow bores them - variation is key
- Mark your big moments and quiet moments, then let cuts, movements, and music flow between them
- Use tools that let you separate music tracks (vocals, drums, bass, piano, guitar, strings) for perfect customization
- Vary your hook's energy: start with tension, pause, add full energy with quick shots, then release into slower shots
- The more you practice creating stories in your head before filming, the better filmmaker you become
- Once audio is locked in, the edit is already halfway done - you just need to add the shots
