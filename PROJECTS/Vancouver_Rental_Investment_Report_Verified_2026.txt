SECTION ONE: MARKET PRICES AND TRENDS

Current Vancouver condo median prices as of July 2026. Downtown trades at seven hundred fifty thousand to eight hundred thousand dollars, down from the nine-hundred-thousand peak in late-2022. Yaletown and Coal Harbour hover around eight-fifty to one-point-one million. Kitsilano sits between eight hundred thousand and one-million-five. Mount Pleasant and Commercial Drive run six-fifty to seven-eighty. East Vancouver near Skytrain stations averages six hundred to seven hundred thousand. Outer areas like Surrey Cambridge range from five-twenty to six-fifty.

The three-year trend tells the full story. The market peaked as mortgage rates surged above five percent in early 2023. Then came a ten-percent average price decline through that year, followed by another three-to-five-percent softening in 2024. The sharpest drops occurred around neighborhoods with heavy new-supply deliveries — Brentwood, Metrotown and Surrey City Centre all saw accelerated absorption pressure from thousands of units completing simultaneously.

Craig Veroni writes directly about this environment: No one consistently predicts the exact bottom or top and many buyers who tried to wait over the last decade simply watched prices rise while they stayed on the sidelines. However he also adds that if you are financially ready and planning to stay five-plus years, buying in 2026 can be a perfectly reasonable decision.

The Bank of Canada held rates around three-point-two-five to three-point-seven-five through mid-2026. This gradual easing prevented panic selling while keeping speculative demand muted.

SECTION TWO: BANK FORECASTS FOR 2026 THROUGH 2031

RBC projects four to seven percent cumulative appreciation in Greater Vancouver condos by end of 2028, then three to five percent annually through 2031. They argue that record immigration targets creating roughly four-hundred-thousand permanent residents yearly plus temporary worker growth sustain baseline demand.

TD Securities is more cautious, expecting only one to three percent annual appreciation for City-of-Vancouver proper through 2030. They specifically flag oversupply around Brentwood and Metrotown as creating two-to-four-percent additional downside risk through late-2027.

Scotiabank is most bullish among the big banks with five-to-eight-percent cumulative gains by end-2027 then steady three-to-four-percent long-term into 2031. They cite the expiry of the foreign buyer ban in April 2026 being replaced with a reduced transfer tax, bringing back some pent-up international demand for luxury product above one-million dollars.

The consensus verdict: Prices have essentially bottomed in the City-of-Vancouver core neighborhoods through mid-2026. Any further declines are modest and localized to oversupplied areas east of Main Street. Waiting two more years might theoretically save you fifty-thousand on a seven-fifty condo but the lost rent collection plus time-value-of-money likely exceeds that savings entirely.

Verified from VancouverHomeSearch.com publication: They quote directly that this pricing environment creates opportunity for disciplined buyers and risk for those still chasing trends. Appreciation can no longer be used to justify overpaying or ignoring poor cash flow. Buyers should assume limited price growth and ensure the transaction works without market rescue.

Expected rent growth of four to five percent annually through 2028 as completions lag population growth significantly.

Critical divergence that creates your opportunity: Rents did not decline during the fifteen-to-twenty-percent price correction. They continued rising three-to-five-percent per year even while condo values dropped. This means rental yields mathematically improve for anyone who buys now.
