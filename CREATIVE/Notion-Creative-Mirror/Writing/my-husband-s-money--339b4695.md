# My Husband's Money

## Summary

A comedic, campy song written from the perspective of an entitled, arrogant husband who lives in a bubble of self-declared divine superiority. The tone is intentionally chaotic and messy, with lyric tweaks focused on preserving that energy while improving flow, rhythm, and character voice.

## Character / Tone

- Entitled, snobby, arrogant, self-obsessed

- Easily distracted and detached from reality

- Treats people as obstacles and luxury as necessity

- Camp, performative, melodramatic, oblivious

## Chat summary

The work focused on small, surgical lyric improvements rather than a full rewrite. The goal was to preserve the intentionally chaotic structure while sharpening rhythm, entitlement, and the character’s oblivious diva voice. “Wonderful” was kept in the opening because it felt softer and more delusionally self-satisfied. The line about diamonds evolved into the approved version: “How’s my hair? Oh — I mustn’t! / My diamonds… Get out of my way!” The chorus line ending was reshaped into the sing-song phrase “As long as I’m spark-ling in ti-a-ra.” For the repeated chorus, the lyrics were changed to a more self-pitying and comedic variation about the husband never buying things for him, ending on “And how can I spark without my ti-a-ra?” The overall character was clarified as a campy, entitled, reality-detached man who sees himself as the center of the universe and treats luxury as basic survival.
