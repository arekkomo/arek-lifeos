---# Imma Nyala

> A dry mockumentary about a faded Egyptian Mau supermodel waiting for her comeback, while her chaotic Toyger adopted child accidentally becomes the real star.
> 
> **Double purpose:** every episode is a directing exercise. Content output = directing school.

---

## One-Line Premise

Imma Nyala is a fake high-end reality series about two cats who do not understand how ridiculous they are.

---

## The Characters

### Nyala — Egyptian Mau
**Role:** Former supermodel. Faded celebrity. Self-appointed matriarch. Waiting for "the call."  
**Personality:** Elegant, dry, sardonic, spoiled, entitled, theatrical, controlled. Status-obsessed.  
**Comedy engine:** Treats tiny domestic cat behaviour as major public scandal or career-defining betrayal.  
**Visual language:** Slow camera. Soft light. Shallow depth of field. Composed framing. Dramatic close-ups. Gentle push-ins. Symmetry where possible.  
**What she never does:** Acknowledge the absurdity. Break composure voluntarily.

**Nyala themes:**
- Former supermodel awaiting her comeback
- Believes she is still the face of luxury
- Treats the home as a mansion / estate
- Has a complicated relationship with Pepe while insisting she is a generous mother
- Gives masterclasses nobody asked for
- Has invented products, perfume, campaigns, and legacy brands around herself
- Speaks as if every moment is part of a memoir

---

### Pepe — Toyger
**Role:** Adopted child. Chaos agent. Accidental influencer. Emotional disruptor.  
**Personality:** Vocal, needy, attention-seeking, chaotic, impulsive, lovable, shameless.  
**Comedy engine:** Ruins Nyala's controlled elegance through sheer unstoppable presence.  
**Visual language:** Handheld. Fast. Quick zooms. Phone-style / vertical framing. Sudden cuts. Disruptive sound design.  
**What he never does:** Understand that he is disrupting anything. He simply exists.

**Pepe themes:**
- Accidental influencer — thinks being loud is a career strategy
- Interrupts Nyala's interviews and elegant moments
- Creates chaos during composed scenes
- Does not understand boundaries
- Turns domestic incidents into viral opportunities

---

### Figgy — Russian Blue
**Role:** Recurring guest. Not a primary cast member.  
**Access constraint:** Limited. Do not build format around Figgy.  
**Works as:** Special guest, mysterious neighbour, refined outsider, rival, cameo.

---

## The Narrator

The narrator is the spine of the show. **Calm. Composed. Slightly elevated. Affectionate but matter-of-fact.** David Attenborough meets Lindsay Duncan in a prestige celebrity documentary.

The narrator never sounds wacky. The narrator never winks at the camera. The absurdity lands because the narrator treats everything as genuinely significant.

## Tone References

- Little Britain — character-based deadpan comedy
- Catherine Tate — character specificity, commitment to the bit
- Reality-TV confessionals — the format used as a weapon
- Prestige celebrity documentary — the tone inverted
- Nature documentary narration — calm seriousness that makes absurdity funnier

**The core mechanism:** serious language + ordinary cat behaviour = comedy.  
**Secondary mechanism:** Nyala's controlled elegance + Pepe's existence = comedy.

---

## What to Avoid

- Generic cute pet content
- A narrator who explains the joke or sounds sarcastic/goofy
- Saying Nyala is "delusional" — **show it, never state it**
- Making Pepe only annoying — he must be lovable and charismatic
- Making Nyala purely mean — elegance and vulnerability must exist beneath the diva
- Cluttered graphic design
- AI/VFX as the main footage source — real cat behaviour is the base

## Recurring Segments

**Nyala's Masterclass** — profound-sounding lessons about elegance, fame, power, or survival. The advice is petty, impractical, or clearly cat behaviour.  
*Topics: how to enter a room, how to ignore someone with dignity, how to maintain mystery while demanding food.*

**Pepe's Influencer Academy** — modern influence through chaos. Lessons on attention, volume, interruption, shamelessness.  
*Topics: if they are not looking, scream. Every closed door is a brand opportunity.*

**MeNyala Perfume** — fake luxury perfume commercial. Overly serious, sensual, absurd. Works as post-credits or standalone.

**Cleopatra / Ancient Icon Flashback** — Nyala has always been worshipped. AI/VFX heightened insert. Externalises her self-image.

**Confessionals** — fake reality-TV interview moments. Handled via narrator interpretation, subtitles, voiceover "translation," or silent stares.

### Imma Nyala — Directing Curriculum

# Directing & Learning Map

> **The approach:** You make episodes because you want to. The learning happens in the doing.  
> This document is not a syllabus. It's a map of what you'll naturally encounter as you make the show — and what to pay attention to when you do.

The ideas come first. Always. You pick an episode because something is funny, because Nyala did something that made you laugh, because you have a narrator line that needs footage around it. The directing awareness rides along — not as a rule, but as a lens you can pick up or put down.

---

## The Natural Learning Loop

Making Imma Nyala will teach you directing because:

1. **You're working with uncontrollable subjects.** Cats don't hit marks. You have to find the shot, not place it. That's exactly what real documentary and observational filmmaking is. It trains your eye.

2. **You're editing against narration.** Choosing which footage fits which line is scene construction. You're doing it every episode.

3. **You have two visually distinct characters.** How you shoot Nyala versus Pepe is a directing decision every single time you pick up the camera. You'll develop instincts for this fast.

4. **Short form demands economy.** Every cut counts in 30 seconds. That's rhythm, pacing, and editorial thinking compressed into something manageable.

The curriculum doesn't drive the episodes. The episodes generate the curriculum.

---

## What You'll Naturally Encounter

These are not assignments. They're things that will come up organically as you make the show. When they do, notice them.

### Shot Language
Without thinking about it, you'll start to understand that a close-up of Nyala does something a wide shot doesn't. That a handheld follow of Pepe feels different from a locked-off frame. You'll develop preferences. Those preferences are your directorial voice.

*Relevant curriculum: Module 01 — Visual Language*

### Character Visual Contrast
Nyala and Pepe need to feel like they come from different shows. You'll figure out how to do this through trial and error. Slow vs fast. Composed vs chaotic. This is one of the most valuable directing skills there is — visual style as character language.

*Relevant curriculum: Module 01 — Visual Language*

### Finding the Story in Behaviour
The footage is always of cats doing cat things. Your job in the edit is to turn that into a story. "She entered. He disrupted. She recovered." That's structure. You'll find it naturally every time you edit.

*Relevant curriculum: Module 02 — Storytelling Structure*

### Writing to Picture
The narrator has to work with what you have. You'll get better at writing lines that fit footage — and better at filming footage that gives you options for narration. These skills will develop together.

*Not in the formal curriculum — this is your own craft.*

### AI as a Storytelling Tool
When the Cleopatra insert or the MeNyala commercial comes, you'll be directing AI-generated images as part of a real sequence. Deciding what they look like, where they sit, how long they hold — that's directing AI, not just prompting it.

*Relevant curriculum: Module 03 — Directing with AI*

## The Golden Rule

> **Nyala footage = slow, deliberate, composed.**  
> **Pepe footage = fast, reactive, kinetic.**  
> Never mix the visual style on the wrong character.

When Nyala and Pepe are in the same shot — decide which visual register the scene belongs to. Usually Nyala's. Pepe is the disruption.

## Nyala — What to Capture

Nyala's comedy comes from her composure. You are always framing her as if she is more significant than she is. She does not perform — she simply exists with gravity.

### Positions and Locations
- [ ] High perch / windowsill / back of armchair — elevated positions that suggest status
- [ ] Sitting in a patch of light — deliberate, chosen, composed
- [ ] At the window / terrace — looking out as if surveying something significant
- [ ] Sitting in the centre of a room — compositionally strong, like she owns the space
- [ ] Near a door — waiting, or recently arrived, or about to depart with intent

### Behaviours to Catch
- [ ] The slow entrance — walking into a room without urgency
- [ ] The sustained stare — looking at nothing, for a very long time
- [ ] The groom — particularly effective after a moment of disruption (implies recovery)
- [ ] The ignore — when Pepe is in the same space, she looks elsewhere deliberately
- [ ] The slow turn — when she finally deigns to look at something or someone
- [ ] The tail curl — any elegant body language that can be framed as intentional
- [ ] The yawn — read as disdain; can be narrated as commentary on the situation
- [ ] The stretch — read as performance or preparation for something
- [ ] Sleeping / resting in an elegant position — can be narrated as "recovery," "strategy," or "scheduled stillness"
- [ ] Eating / drinking — frame as she is the subject, not the bowl

### Shot Types for Nyala
- Close-up on face / eyes — emotion, gravity, disdain
- Medium — her full body in a chosen position
- Slow push-in on any static moment — the classic "something is happening internally"
- Wide establishing her in the room — her relationship to the space
- Insert shots: paw, tail, detail — for cutaways and editorial texture

---

## Pepe — What to Capture

Pepe's comedy comes from his volume and his certainty. He is fully committed to everything he does. Nothing is half-hearted. Film him with the energy he brings — match it.

### Positions and Locations
- [ ] Doorways — arriving, demanding, blocked, staring through
- [ ] Mid-movement — running, jumping, landing, changing direction
- [ ] On top of / next to Nyala — physically imposing on her space
- [ ] Directly into camera — his best and most confident moments
- [ ] Low to the ground, looking up — adds chaos and drama
- [ ] In motion through a frame Nyala occupies — the classic disruption shot

### Behaviours to Catch
- [ ] Vocalisations — yells, chirps, demands, commentary; film the face during these
- [ ] The run-through — entering and exiting a composed frame at speed
- [ ] The yell at a door / wall / object — his relationship to inanimate obstacles
- [ ] The demand — at the food bowl, at Nyala, at the camera, at nothing
- [ ] The interrupt — entering Nyala's moment at the worst possible time
- [ ] The stare into camera — direct, unbroken, inappropriately intense
- [ ] The sudden stop — running at full speed, then frozen, looking at something
- [ ] The play — physically chaotic, great for Influencer Academy segments
- [ ] Pepe following Nyala — the opposite of what she wants
- [ ] Pepe attempting to groom Nyala / be near Nyala — her reaction is the comedy

### Shot Types for Pepe
- Handheld follow — stay with him, don't fight him
- Quick zoom in — from medium to close-up, when he does something sudden
- Vertical framing — phone-style, social-media coded
- Low angle looking up — makes him feel chaotic and large
- Wide handheld — for sequences where he's moving through a space
- Direct-to-camera close-up — for the Influencer Academy moments

## The Directing Session Habit

The filming session does not need to be long. 15–30 minutes of intentional filming with the Directing Focus active is more valuable than an hour of general footage.

**Minimum viable session:**
1. Set up one composed Nyala shot and hold it for 3–5 minutes
2. Follow Pepe through one sequence with intention
3. Get one shot you didn't plan — react to what they give you

That is enough for one short episode.



### Imma Nyala — Voice Direction

# Narrator Style Guide

> The narrator is the spine of Imma Nyala. Everything else can be chaotic. The narrator never is.

---

## The Voice

**Reference energy:** David Attenborough narrating a prestige nature documentary. But instead of the Serengeti, it is a living room in Vancouver. The affection and gravity are identical.

**Secondary reference:** A calm, slightly melancholy BBC documentary about a faded celebrity — Lindsay Duncan reading a profile of someone who once mattered enormously.

**The key:** The narrator genuinely believes in the importance of what they are describing. The comedy is not in the narrator's tone. The comedy is in the gap between the narrator's tone and what is actually on screen.

---

## Rules

**1. Never wink at the audience.**  
The narrator does not find this funny. The narrator finds this significant, moving, and worthy of documentation. If the narrator sounds even slightly sarcastic, the comedy collapses.

**2. Never explain the joke.**  
If Nyala is grooming herself after a moment of dignity has been destroyed, the narrator does not say "she appeared unaffected." The narrator says something that makes the grooming sound like a strategic decision of the highest order.

**3. Never say "delusional."**  
Never say Nyala is confused, mistaken, or out of touch. The narrator does not see it that way. The narrator's frame is that Nyala is correct, and the world has not yet caught up.

**4. Use specific, slightly elevated language.**  
Not florid. Not purple. But deliberate. The narrator chooses words that sound like they belong in a documentary about someone who matters enormously.

**5. Treat time as elastic.**  
The narrator can pause on ordinary moments for much longer than they deserve. A cat sitting in a chair becomes an extended meditation on stillness and preparation.

**6. Never rush.**  
The narrator has all the time in the world. The narrator has decided that this is important enough to be slow.

---

## Tone Register: The Spectrum

The narrator operates in a very specific band. Not at either extreme.

```
PARODY ←————————————————————————————————→ SINCERE

[Winking, Obviously Ironic]                [No Comedy At All]
          ↑
     NARRATOR LIVES HERE
     
Serious enough that the absurdity lands.
Not so serious that it's just pretentious.
```

The comedy comes from the narrator being just slightly too serious for the content. One note past what's necessary. Never two notes past.

---

## Sentence Structure

Narrator sentences tend to:

- Begin with the subject, stated simply and fully: *"Nyala."* or *"She had arrived at this conclusion slowly."*
- Use the passive voice occasionally for extra gravitas: *"It was decided."* or *"This had been anticipated."*
- Pause with commas where speech would not normally pause: *"She had, in her assessment, prepared thoroughly."*
- End on a full stop, not a question: the narrator has conclusions, not uncertainties
- Avoid contractions: *"she had not"* rather than *"she hadn't"*
- Occasionally use a single-word sentence as punctuation: *"She waited. Strategically."*

## Sample Lines

### Character Introduction

> "Nyala. Former face of the industry. A presence that, once encountered, left its mark permanently on those fortunate enough to witness it. She has, since the conclusion of her public era, been resting. Strategically."

> "She had not, as some had suggested, retired. She had simply been selecting the appropriate moment. This had been ongoing for some time."

> "Pepe. Adopted. Energetic. Apparently under the impression that enthusiasm is a professional skill."

> "He had been with Nyala for three years. She considered this, most days, a demonstration of her generosity."

---

### Describing Ordinary Cat Behaviour

**Nyala staring out a window:**
> "From her position at the terrace window, she observed the city below. It had, she had decided, not yet reached its full potential. She remained available for consultation."

**Nyala grooming:**
> "The preparation was meticulous. It always was. One does not return to relevance without attending to the fundamentals."

**Nyala ignoring Pepe:**
> "She had heard him. This was evident. She chose, as she so often did, to respond with the more powerful register: silence. Extended. Deliberate. Devastating."

**Pepe yelling at a door:**
> "His feelings on this matter were clear and consistently communicated. The door had not, to date, responded to his position. He was not deterred."

**Pepe running through frame:**
> "He passed through the room at considerable speed, contributing what he had always described — to no one, in particular — as 'energy.'"

**Nyala sitting in a patch of sunlight:**
> "She had selected this position two hours earlier. The light, she noted, was finally doing what she had asked of it."

**Nyala and Pepe in the same frame:**
> "For a moment, the two of them occupied the same space. Nyala regarded this as temporary. Pepe appeared to regard it as a collaboration."

> "The morning had passed without the call. She did not mention this. She did not need to. It was understood."

> "Something had shifted. Whether the shift was external or internal was, at this point, a distinction without significance."

## What the Narrator Never Says

- "Hilariously..." — the narrator does not find things hilarious
- "...or so she thought" — too obvious a comedy beat, too winking
- "Meanwhile..." — too casual; use *"At the same time"* or simply cut without narration
- "Of course..." — implies irony; the narrator has no irony
- "Somehow..." — implies disbelief; the narrator believes everything
- "As one might expect..." — condescending to the subject
- "Nyala seemed..." — the narrator does not guess; the narrator knows

---

## Practical Recording Notes

- Pace: slow down to 70% of what feels natural. Then slow down 10% more.
- Breath: take a breath before key lines. The pause is part of the delivery.
- Pitch: slightly lower than your normal speaking register
- Volume: conversational, not projected. The mic is close. The narrator is intimate.
- Tone: affectionate but not warm. Observational but not cold. The narrator cares about this. They simply do not show it in the conventional way.
