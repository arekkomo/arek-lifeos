# Filming Checklist

> Film with intention. Not everything — the right things.  
> The cats will not perform on command. Your job is to be ready when they do.

---

## Before You Film

- [ ] Read the Directing Focus for this episode
- [ ] Know the Constraint (what rules apply to this session)
- [ ] Have the Scene Brief in mind (even loosely)
- [ ] Phone / camera charged and ready to go
- [ ] Know which location you're using as the primary set
- [ ] Stabilise if you're shooting Nyala (tripod, surface, or braced position)
- [ ] Know if you're in handheld mode (Pepe) or locked-off mode (Nyala)

---

## The Golden Rule

> **Nyala footage = slow, deliberate, composed.**  
> **Pepe footage = fast, reactive, kinetic.**  
> Never mix the visual style on the wrong character.

When Nyala and Pepe are in the same shot — decide which visual register the scene belongs to. Usually Nyala's. Pepe is the disruption.

---

## Nyala — What to Capture

Nyala's comedy comes from her composure. You are always framing her as if she is more significant than she is. She does not perform — she simply exists with gravity.

### Positions and Locations
- [ ] High perch / windowsill / back of armchair — elevated positions that suggest status
- [ ] Sitting in a patch of light — deliberate, chosen, composed
- [ ] At the window / terrace — looking out as if surveying something significant
- [ ] Sitting in the centre of a room — compositionally strong, like she owns the space
- [ ] Near a door — waiting, or recently arrived, or about to depart with intent

### Behaviours to Catch
- [ ] The slow entrance — walking into a room without urgency
- [ ] The sustained stare — looking at nothing, for a very long time
- [ ] The groom — particularly effective after a moment of disruption (implies recovery)
- [ ] The ignore — when Pepe is in the same space, she looks elsewhere deliberately
- [ ] The slow turn — when she finally deigns to look at something or someone
- [ ] The tail curl — any elegant body language that can be framed as intentional
- [ ] The yawn — read as disdain; can be narrated as commentary on the situation
- [ ] The stretch — read as performance or preparation for something
- [ ] Sleeping / resting in an elegant position — can be narrated as "recovery," "strategy," or "scheduled stillness"
- [ ] Eating / drinking — frame as she is the subject, not the bowl

### Shot Types for Nyala
- Close-up on face / eyes — emotion, gravity, disdain
- Medium — her full body in a chosen position
- Slow push-in on any static moment — the classic "something is happening internally"
- Wide establishing her in the room — her relationship to the space
- Insert shots: paw, tail, detail — for cutaways and editorial texture

## Pepe — What to Capture

Pepe's comedy comes from his volume and his certainty. He is fully committed to everything he does. Nothing is half-hearted. Film him with the energy he brings — match it.

### Positions and Locations
- [ ] Doorways — arriving, demanding, blocked, staring through
- [ ] Mid-movement — running, jumping, landing, changing direction
- [ ] On top of / next to Nyala — physically imposing on her space
- [ ] Directly into camera — his best and most confident moments
- [ ] Low to the ground, looking up — adds chaos and drama
- [ ] In motion through a frame Nyala occupies — the classic disruption shot

### Behaviours to Catch
- [ ] Vocalisations — yells, chirps, demands, commentary; film the face during these
- [ ] The run-through — entering and exiting a composed frame at speed
- [ ] The yell at a door / wall / object — his relationship to inanimate obstacles
- [ ] The demand — at the food bowl, at Nyala, at the camera, at nothing
- [ ] The interrupt — entering Nyala's moment at the worst possible time
- [ ] The stare into camera — direct, unbroken, inappropriately intense
- [ ] The sudden stop — running at full speed, then frozen, looking at something
- [ ] The play — physically chaotic, great for Influencer Academy segments
- [ ] Pepe following Nyala — the opposite of what she wants
- [ ] Pepe attempting to groom Nyala / be near Nyala — her reaction is the comedy

### Shot Types for Pepe
- Handheld follow — stay with him, don't fight him
- Quick zoom in — from medium to close-up, when he does something sudden
- Vertical framing — phone-style, social-media coded
- Low angle looking up — makes him feel chaotic and large
- Wide handheld — for sequences where he's moving through a space
- Direct-to-camera close-up — for the Influencer Academy moments

## Location Reframes — The Mansion

Every domestic location gets a grand title. Know your estate names before filming.

| Actual Location | Estate Name |
|----------------|-------------|
| Living room | The main salon / the reception hall |
| Bedroom | The private suite / the west chamber |
| Kitchen | The provisions wing |
| Bathroom | The wellness suite / the restoration chamber |
| Hallway | The east corridor |
| Windowsill | The observation terrace / the east-facing terrace |
| Patio / garden | The private grounds / the outdoor estate |
| Any cat tree | The central atelier / the viewing platform |
| Favourite chair | The signature chair / the principal seat |

Use these in narration. The comedy comes from the narrator using these terms with complete sincerity.

## Interaction Shots — Nyala and Pepe Together

These are gold. Capture any moment where both cats are in frame, especially:

- [ ] Pepe approaching Nyala while she ignores him
- [ ] Pepe yelling near Nyala — her stillness beside his noise
- [ ] Any physical contact / proximity — Nyala's tolerance or lack of it
- [ ] Both looking in the same direction — rare, precious, narrate as "a moment of alignment"
- [ ] Pepe interrupting a Nyala moment — the core disruption structure
- [ ] Nyala leaving after Pepe arrives — the dismissal

## Figgy (Russian Blue) — If Available

- Treat as a guest appearance only
- Film in a composed, slightly mysterious register — neither Nyala's gravity nor Pepe's chaos
- Works best as: the rival, the outsider, the brief visit
- Do not build an episode around Figgy — cameo and supporting only

## After the Session

- [ ] Review footage immediately — identify the usable clips while it's fresh
- [ ] Write brief clip descriptions or timecodes in the Episode Template
- [ ] Note anything you wanted but didn't get (to look for next session)
- [ ] If you got something unexpected that's funny — note it and build narration around it

---

## The Directing Session Habit

The filming session does not need to be long. 15–30 minutes of intentional filming with the Directing Focus active is more valuable than an hour of general footage.

**Minimum viable session:**
1. Set up one composed Nyala shot and hold it for 3–5 minutes
2. Follow Pepe through one sequence with intention
3. Get one shot you didn't plan — react to what they give you

That is enough for one short episode.
