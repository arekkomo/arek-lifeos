# Directing & Learning Map

> **The approach:** You make episodes because you want to. The learning happens in the doing.  
> This document is not a syllabus. It's a map of what you'll naturally encounter as you make the show — and what to pay attention to when you do.

The ideas come first. Always. You pick an episode because something is funny, because Nyala did something that made you laugh, because you have a narrator line that needs footage around it. The directing awareness rides along — not as a rule, but as a lens you can pick up or put down.

---

## The Natural Learning Loop

Making Imma Nyala will teach you directing because:

1. **You're working with uncontrollable subjects.** Cats don't hit marks. You have to find the shot, not place it. That's exactly what real documentary and observational filmmaking is. It trains your eye.

2. **You're editing against narration.** Choosing which footage fits which line is scene construction. You're doing it every episode.

3. **You have two visually distinct characters.** How you shoot Nyala versus Pepe is a directing decision every single time you pick up the camera. You'll develop instincts for this fast.

4. **Short form demands economy.** Every cut counts in 30 seconds. That's rhythm, pacing, and editorial thinking compressed into something manageable.

The curriculum doesn't drive the episodes. The episodes generate the curriculum.

---

## What You'll Naturally Encounter

These are not assignments. They're things that will come up organically as you make the show. When they do, notice them.

### Shot Language
Without thinking about it, you'll start to understand that a close-up of Nyala does something a wide shot doesn't. That a handheld follow of Pepe feels different from a locked-off frame. You'll develop preferences. Those preferences are your directorial voice.

*Relevant curriculum: Module 01 — Visual Language*

### Character Visual Contrast
Nyala and Pepe need to feel like they come from different shows. You'll figure out how to do this through trial and error. Slow vs fast. Composed vs chaotic. This is one of the most valuable directing skills there is — visual style as character language.

*Relevant curriculum: Module 01 — Visual Language*

### Finding the Story in Behaviour
The footage is always of cats doing cat things. Your job in the edit is to turn that into a story. "She entered. He disrupted. She recovered." That's structure. You'll find it naturally every time you edit.

*Relevant curriculum: Module 02 — Storytelling Structure*

### Writing to Picture
The narrator has to work with what you have. You'll get better at writing lines that fit footage — and better at filming footage that gives you options for narration. These skills will develop together.

*Not in the formal curriculum — this is your own craft.*

### AI as a Storytelling Tool
When the Cleopatra insert or the MeNyala commercial comes, you'll be directing AI-generated images as part of a real sequence. Deciding what they look like, where they sit, how long they hold — that's directing AI, not just prompting it.

*Relevant curriculum: Module 03 — Directing with AI*

## Loose Episode Progression (Not a Schedule)

These are episode ideas loosely ordered from simpler to more ambitious. Do them in whatever order makes sense. Skip any that don't interest you. Come back to them later.

**Simpler — good starting points:**
- "The Entrance" — one clear moment, a few shots, a narrator line
- "The Estate" — location tour, playful titles, minimal story needed
- "Influencer Academy: Lesson One" — Pepe-led, fast, kinetic, fun to shoot
- "Masterclass: How to Enter a Room" — Nyala-led, composed, comedic contrast

**Mid-complexity — once you have some footage habits:**
- "The Wait" — sustained mood, requires patience with the frame
- "Motherhood" — needs a genuine Nyala/Pepe interaction sequence
- "MeNyala" — short, but needs deliberate visual choices for the commercial aesthetic

**More ambitious — when you're ready:**
- "The Call" (Pilot) — full arc, multiple segments, Cleopatra insert, 7–9 min
- "Cleopatra" — AI/VFX integration into real footage
- "The Campaign" — AI-generated imagery as editorial material

## After Each Episode — Optional Reflection

When you finish an episode, if you feel like it, write a quick note on what you noticed. Not a grade. Just an observation.

*"The close-up at the end worked better than I thought."*  
*"I need to get lower when I film Pepe — the angle was wrong."*  
*"The narration line was funnier once I cut to the wide."*

These observations, over time, become your directing instincts. Log them in [[Episode-Log]] if you want to keep them. Or just let them accumulate in your head. Either works.
