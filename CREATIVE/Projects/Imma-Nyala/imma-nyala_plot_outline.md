# Genre Episode Map

> **The structure:** One episode per week, 3–4 minutes. Same world, same characters, different genre each time.  
> The joke is that Nyala and Pepe are always themselves — but the filmmaking around them shifts completely.  
> A horror Imma Nyala episode is both a horror filmmaking exercise and a comedy about treating cats as horror subjects.

Each entry covers:
- The **genre or technique** being explored
- What it teaches you about filmmaking
- A **premise** — the Imma Nyala spin on that genre
- What to look at before filming (reference, not homework)

---

## The Episode Map

---

### EP-01 — Comedy
**Technique:** Shooting comedy — timing, contrast, reaction shots, the rule of threes  
**The premise:** Nyala enters a room. The narrator treats it as a historic public appearance. Pepe runs through and ruins it.  
**What you're learning:** Comedy is in the edit. The gap between the setup and the punchline is a timing decision. Reaction shots are everything — Nyala's non-reaction is funnier than any joke.  
**Watch before filming:** Any Buster Keaton short. Notice how much work the reaction (or non-reaction) does. No dialogue needed.  
**File:** [[EP-01-The-Entrance]]

---

### EP-02 — Horror
**Technique:** Shooting horror — tension through silence, darkness, framing, sound design, withholding  
**The premise:** Something is wrong in the estate. Nyala has noticed. She will not say what. Pepe has not noticed and this is somehow more disturbing.  
**What you're learning:** Horror is about what you don't show. Long holds on nothing. Sound before image. The wide shot that is just slightly off-center. The close-up that arrives too slowly.  
**The comedy:** The horror of a cat staring at a wall in the dark is already there. You just have to treat it seriously.  
**Watch before filming:** The opening of *The Shining* or any Ari Aster film — notice how long they hold a frame before anything happens. That patience is the technique.

---

### EP-03 — Nature Documentary
**Technique:** Observational filmmaking — the Attenborough mode. Wide establishing shots, slow reveals, scientific language for mundane behaviour  
**The premise:** A nature documentary crew has gained rare access to Nyala's private estate. They observe, carefully, from a respectful distance.  
**What you're learning:** Distance changes everything. The wide shot that watches rather than participates. Narration that describes behaviour with scientific wonder. The comedy is the same as the whole show — but pushed further.  
**Watch before filming:** Any Attenborough BBC series opener. Notice the patience, the long lens, the respect for the subject's space.

---

### EP-04 — Romance / Melodrama
**Technique:** Shooting longing — soft light, slow motion, close-ups on small details, music as emotional driver  
**The premise:** Nyala has received an invitation. She has not confirmed whether she will attend. She has, however, spent forty minutes near the window.  
**What you're learning:** Emotion in filmmaking is often in the details — the paw, the angle of light, the slight movement. Slow motion and a good music choice can make any mundane cat moment feel like a devastatingly romantic scene.  
**The comedy:** Pure bathos. The more beautiful you make it look, the funnier it is that it's a cat sitting by a window.  
**Watch before filming:** Any Wong Kar-wai film — *In the Mood for Love* particularly. Watch how he uses slow motion, shadows, and close-ups of incidental details to generate overwhelming feeling.

---

### EP-05 — Thriller
**Technique:** Building suspense through pacing and cutting — the Hitchcock model. Information control. The audience knows more than the character (or less).  
**The premise:** Something has been moved. Nyala knows. Pepe doesn't know she knows. Or perhaps he does.  
**What you're learning:** Suspense is created by what the audience knows and when they know it. Cross-cutting between two characters who don't know they're in the same scene. The insert shot that gives too much information. The cut that comes too early.  
**Watch before filming:** The shower scene in *Psycho* — not for the violence but for the cutting rhythm. Or the crop-duster sequence in *North by Northwest* — suspense through geography and patience.

---

### EP-06 — Fashion Film / Editorial
**Technique:** The fashion film mode — slow motion, extreme close-ups on texture, abstract framing, music-driven editing  
**The premise:** A day in the life of Nyala, shot as a luxury fashion editorial. Every ordinary moment becomes iconic. Every blink is a campaign image.  
**What you're learning:** Fashion films have almost no narrative. They're pure visual rhythm and mood. The edit is music-first — image cut to beat, not to story. This is a useful counter-training to narrative editing.  
**The comedy:** Shot beautifully enough, Nyala grooming herself becomes a perfume ad.  
**Watch before filming:** Any high-end fashion film by Nick Knight or Quentin Jones. Or a Chanel or Dior short film. Notice the ratio of narrative to pure image.

---

### EP-07 — True Crime
**Technique:** The true crime documentary mode — dramatic re-enactments, interview cutaways, conspiratorial narration, evidence inserts  
**The premise:** "The Incident of the 14th." Something happened. Nyala has declined to comment. Pepe was seen in the vicinity.  
**What you're learning:** True crime is about framing — the same footage presented as evidence rather than observation changes everything. This is a lesson in how narration and framing create meaning from neutral footage.  
**The comedy:** The incident was almost certainly something trivial — a knocked-over glass, a missing treat. The gravity of the production is the joke.

---

### EP-08 — Infomercial / Commercial
**Technique:** Shooting product — the infomercial and fake commercial mode. Demonstration, testimony, problem/solution structure  
**The premise:** MeNyala. The perfume. The masterclass. Any of Nyala's product ventures.  
**What you're learning:** Commercial filmmaking has its own grammar — the product close-up, the testimonial cut, the before/after structure. It's useful to understand this language because parody requires fluency.  
**Watch before filming:** Watch one real luxury perfume commercial and one low-budget TV infomercial back to back. The contrast is the tutorial.

---

### EP-09 — Sci-Fi / Surreal
**Technique:** Using AI and visual effects as storytelling tools — not spectacle, but meaning  
**The premise:** Nyala's memories. Or Nyala's vision of the future. Something that cannot be real footage — the Cleopatra sequence, the campaign, the fantasy.  
**What you're learning:** AI-generated imagery works best when it's doing something real footage cannot. This episode is where you practice integrating the two — finding the cut between the real and the surreal that feels motivated rather than arbitrary.

---

### EP-10 — Found Footage / Amateur
**Technique:** The low-fi aesthetic — handheld, direct to camera, unpolished, intimate  
**The premise:** Pepe has found the camera. This is his footage. He has opinions.  
**What you're learning:** Choosing to shoot rough is a deliberate aesthetic decision. The found footage mode works because it feels unmediated — the opposite of everything Nyala stands for. Pepe's episode should feel like a completely different show.  
**The comedy:** The contrast with Nyala's cinematic gravitas is maximum here.

---

### EP-11 — Western
**Technique:** The Leone model — extreme close-ups, landscape, silence, the standoff  
**The premise:** Nyala and Pepe. The food bowl. There can only be one.  
**What you're learning:** Leone's westerns are about the face and the landscape — nothing in between. Extreme close-ups of eyes, then a wide of the space between the characters, then another close-up. The edit creates tension that has nothing to do with action.

---

### EP-12 — Musical / Rhythm Edit
**Technique:** Music-driven editing — cutting to beat, using rhythm as narrative structure  
**The premise:** A day in the estate, cut entirely to music. No narration. Events follow the rhythm of the track.  
**What you're learning:** Music-first editing forces you to think about rhythm before story. It's a different brain than narrative editing — and it makes you better at both.

## How to Use This Map

Pick the genre that interests you most right now. Or pick the one that feels funniest given what Nyala and Pepe have been doing lately. 

Before filming, spend 20 minutes with the reference material. Not to study it — to absorb the visual language of the genre so it's active in your head when you pick up the camera.

The full pipeline for each episode:

```
WRITING        → premise, narrator draft, scene brief
PREPRODUCTION  → shot ideas, location, what you're looking for
FILMING        → 15–30 min session with the genre in mind
POST           → edit, narration recording, music, titles, any AI/VFX
REFLECT        → one paragraph on what the genre taught you
```
