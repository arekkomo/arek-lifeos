# Chaotic Baking — Music Video Script

## Visual Approach

Every frame is a dual image. What reads as pastry work on one read can be intimacy on another. Framing, lighting, and the *distance of the camera* decide which it is. The two performers' closeness does most of the telling — we never need to spell it.

**Aesthetic:** Industrial kitchen energy. Exposed surfaces, raw light. The set is a blank room that *could* be a bakery or a bedroom — stripped down to work surface, bare bulbs on cables, flour-dusted floor that could just be something else scattered across hardwood. Costume: both in dark, tactile fabrics. Skin appears where heat and friction earn it.

---

## ACT I — Setup (Verse 1 + Pre-Chorus | ~0:00–0:45)

**Shot 1 — Wide.** Bare room. One work-surface center frame. Empty until the camera drifts in. The performer with hands comes first — knuckles pressing into surface, testing it. Slow push-in. The second performer enters from off-frame; we only see one arm resting against the wall behind.

**Shot 2 — Close on hands.** Fingertips drag across a wide sheet of something pale and pliable. Stretches thin between two sets of hands at its edges. Camera doesn't show faces yet — you judge only by touch. Pull tight, let snap back. Over and over. Work-surface doubles as tension field.

**Shot 3 — Profile.** Two torsos close. One leans in; the other resists — a micro-shrug away that turns into an arch instead. Heat. Not gentle, not violent. *Earned.* Camera tracks along their spines like it's tracing a crease line.

**Shot 4 — Overhead shot.** Both pairs of hands on the same surface now. Flour/dust scattered in random paths between them. One set presses down; the other pulls back. The tension is visible in the way the material *between* them stretches and catches light.

**Shot 5 — Tight frame.** Pre-chorus hit on "Press me down." Both forearms brace into frame simultaneously, shoulders tight, breath catching in unison across a shared exhale held too long. Cut to black for half a beat before the chorus explodes.

---

## ACT II — The Collision (Chorus + Verse 2 | ~0:45–1:30)

**Shot 6 — Chorus opens with full frame.** Both performers now fully in shot, close enough that their arms cross and un-cross as they work the same space. Industrial pendant light swings just out of focus in the foreground. Camera rocks slightly, like it's breathing. Hands meet hands meet surface meet skin — indistinguishable from below the forehead down. Everything is motion.

**Shot 7 — Slow-mo insert.** What looks like pastry tearing or folding at speed catches a shaft of warm light mid-motion. The material (dough? fabric? neither is confirmed) stretches to its limit, then snaps back with a white flash. This is your money shot for the chorus hook.

**Shot 8 — Close on faces — first time in the song.** Both performers finally in frame. Faces close up. Foreheads nearly touching. Eyes don't look at each other; they're both locked downward on what their hands are doing together. The tension is worse because they won't meet it.

**Shot 9 — Verse 2 hits.** The camera drops to table level. We see everything from the surface upward — hands, forearms, elbows, the lowest edge of a jawline, knees pressing down. You can place these hands anywhere and the frame works. That's the point. Work becomes caress becomes argument becomes surrender. Camera tracks laterally like it's being dragged along the surface between them.

**Shot 10 — Split attention.** One performer turns their face toward camera for a bar, eyes finally locking with lens. The other keeps working — hands still busy, still pulling, still *doing something* — barely acknowledging that someone is watching. Then they break and look too. Hold for one beat after the lyric ends.

---

## ACT III — The Turn (Pre-Chorus 2 + Bridge | ~1:30–2:30)

**Shot 11 — Pre-chorus repeat.** Same setup as Shot 5, but now both performers are *looking at each other*. That's the first shift. They've earned the eye contact through eight minutes of fighting each other over the same surface. Cut to black before the chorus hits again.

**Shot 12 — Chorus repeat 2x.** Full choreography: all hands, all bodies in motion together now. Where before there was resistance, now there's coordination. Different angles but same frame density. It reads as collaboration for the first time. The camera moves slower here on purpose — let the viewer sit in the relief of it.

**Shot 13 — Bridge opens: silence moment.** Music drops (or pulls back). Wide still shot. Both performers stand at opposite ends of the work surface, hands resting flat, breathing hard. Not talking. Just *knowing* what they just did was something they couldn't un-do. Warm overhead light catches sweat on collarbones and shoulders. Dust everywhere — on surface, floor, clothes, skin. The room has been used up.

**Shot 14 — Close-up sequence (the "turn"):**
- Face tilted back slightly, eyes half-closed but not resting — *listening* to something only they feel.
- Hand running slowly down a forearm that's still warm from the tension of earlier shots. Palms and fingertips do what was previously done with fists.
- A shoulder exposed by fabric pulled aside — one person helping, not taking. Gentle enough that it doesn't need to be explicit because context did the work.
- Eyes open at last. Both performers on camera looking at *each other* now. This is earned eye contact. The bridge line: *"Turn me gentle, hold me slow."* Delivered without a word needed.

**Shot 15 — Final chorus opens.** Music crashes back in. Camera starts close on one face and pulls backward through every frame of the song until you're at wide again and can see everything they've made together — the room, the surface, both of them standing flush against each other now without trying. The movement is slow even though the music is loud. Contrast is the point.

---

## ACT IV — Resolution (Outro | ~2:30–end)

**Shot 16 — Close on a still hand.** Resting flat on the used surface. Flour/dust patterns visible in skin creases. The fight is over. The hand doesn't move for two bars of music — letting the viewer sit in stillness after chaos you just watched.

**Shot 17 — Pull back slowly.** Both performers now just leaning against each other without framing it that way. Not quite embracing, not quite apart. Somewhere exactly between those states. The work surface is marked and used up. Warm light on everything.

**Shot 18 — Final hold.** Wide frame. They're in frame but barely occupying it at this point — smaller than they were mid-song, which makes the emotional arc complete. Light shifts warm gold across the scene. The last visual note matches the last lyric: *"Just enough."* Hold on the wide three full seconds past silence. Don't cut until you want to.

Fade out in darkness. Not white-out. Not iris. Just slow fade — like a room settling after people have left it.

---

## Production Notes

- **Camera language during chaos:** handheld, tight frames, rapid cuts
- **Camera language during resolution:** tripod or gimbal, wide frames, slow push/pull
- **Color palette:** Cool tones (blue/grey) in Act I-II shifting to warm amber by Act III
- **Key prop that does double duty:** the work surface. Everything reads differently depending on whether you see what's above it or below it
- **Wardrobe:** Dark, textured fabrics that read as kitchen-work until warm light catches skin underneath
- **No dialogue.** The music and imagery carry everything
