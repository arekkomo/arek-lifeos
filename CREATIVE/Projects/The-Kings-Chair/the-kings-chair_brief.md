---# The King's Chair

**Working title.**

## One-line concept
At an absurdly solemn marriage summit, a king tries to keep two hostile rival kings from starting a war over his daughter—while he remains trapped in his chair, terrified that allowing a second fart could be disastrously wet.

## Core philosophy / emotional intent
Treat a ridiculous bodily emergency with the ceremonial gravity of international diplomacy. The father’s physical containment becomes the visual form of his need to control everyone: the rival kings, the marriage negotiation, and his daughter’s future. His daughter quietly tries to escape the arrangement, but the men misread her movements as political provocations and repeatedly pull her back into the conflict.

## Format grammar
- Portrait-first, 9:16 vertical micro drama.
- Point of view stays with the King-Father; daughter and rival kings create escalating perimeter chaos.
- Total seriousness in performance, ceremony, and cinematography; no explicit gross-out image or aftermath.
- Episode engine: an attempted concealment reads as favouritism or insult, escalating the summit while the daughter nears an exit.

## Established story world
- Two rival kings are competing for the daughter’s hand; she wants neither.
- The father hosts and mediates a fragile marriage/peace summit.
- Neither rival attributes the first fart to the father. His increasingly anxious cover-ups cause each side to suspect the other of an insult, threat, or alliance.
- The daughter tries to slip away but is accidentally drawn back into the kings’ conflict.

## Episode 1 opening — agreed direction
1. A grand crowd cheers as the two rival kings complete their ceremonial handshake beneath the father’s watch.
2. The King-Father is about to announce peace. Silence falls; every camera and courtier waits.
3. A small, unmistakable sound breaks the silence. The father freezes.
4. Neither king looks at him. **King One hears it as a deliberate insult—a mocking sound aimed at humiliating him at the moment of peace. King Two instantly assumes King One made the sound and is now trying to frame him; his reflex is to read bad faith into every gesture. They are so absorbed in this mutual accusation that neither even considers the King-Father.**
5. The father becomes invisible at his own summit: trapped in his chair, unable to confess, and powerless to interrupt the diplomatic catastrophe unfolding around him.
6. His daughter recognises the opening and quietly heads for the exit.
7. A royal guard, summoned by one of the rival kings as the accusation escalates, bursts through the main doors. In the confusion he physically ushers/pushes her back to the centre of the room—the exact public focal point she was trying to escape.

## Father’s comic-tragic engine
The King-Father is so afraid of offending either side or making the summit worse that he does nothing. His inaction is not neutral: every silence, frozen expression, and refusal to correct the misunderstanding becomes evidence that one rival king is being favoured. His physical paralysis mirrors his diplomatic cowardice; by attempting to contain every risk, he steadily makes the conflict—and his daughter’s entrapment—worse.

## Ending rule — interruption instead of release
The father never decisively acts. His recurring promise is that he is *about to* stand, speak, protect his daughter, or finally take responsibility—then a fresh escalation interrupts him and lets him retreat into paralysis. This is not a redemption arc; it is an escalating **edge** for the viewer. Each interruption makes his eventual action feel more imminent while proving he will choose safety, propriety, and self-preservation again.

## Daughter’s recurring comic engine
She is not trying to win the political game. Her only objective is to leave it. In every episode, she finds a plausible exit; an accident born of the rival kings’ conflict returns her to its centre, usually as an unwilling symbol, witness, hostage, or presumed choice. Her repeated near-escapes give the series a clean secondary rhythm against the father’s escalating physical containment.

## Status
**Development — next:** define what each rival king thinks the sound means, and the daughter’s concrete escape route.

### The King's Chair — Character Bible

# Character Profiles

## King-Father
- **Story function:** Host of the peace summit; the unseen source of the first disaster.
- **Objective:** Preserve ceremony, avoid offending either king, and conceal his bodily emergency.
- **Comic engine:** Inaction. Every time he might stand, explain, or protect his daughter, fear freezes him; his silence worsens the political reading.
- **Performance:** Ceremonially composed face under mounting private terror; tiny eye movements, clenched hands, rigid posture, held breath.
- **Visual continuity:** Heavy formal royal robes; seated on the throne for most of the season; never visually explicit about the fart.

## Daughter / Princess
- **Story function:** The human stake and recurring escape engine.
- **Objective:** Leave the summit and avoid marriage to either rival king.
- **Comic engine:** Every practical attempt to leave gets accidentally reinterpreted as political allegiance, then physically returns her to the centre of conflict.
- **Performance:** Direct, alert, impatient with nonsense; not a chess-master and not a passive victim.
- **Visual continuity:** Practical travel-ready layer beneath formal royal dress; a small, repeatable escape item may be established later.

## King One
- **Story function:** Rival suitor; reads the fart as a personal, public humiliation.
- **Objective:** Win the Daughter and never appear ridiculed or weak.
- **Comic engine:** Vanity turns a minor social breach into an intolerable insult.
- **Performance:** Offended stillness before theatrical outrage.

## King Two
- **Story function:** Rival suitor; instantly assumes King One is framing him.
- **Objective:** Avoid being manipulated, blamed, or outmanoeuvred by King One.
- **Comic engine:** Reflexive paranoia; every gesture is evidence of bad faith.
- **Performance:** Suspicious, quick, certain without proof.

## Fake Superhero
- **Story function:** The Daughter’s apparent saviour and the episode-two revelation that makes her position worse.
- **Objective:** Impress the Daughter and preserve the fantasy of being a superhero.
- **Comic engine:** He performs confidence and heroism flawlessly until the court pays close attention; the costume and persona are a transparent dating scam.
- **Performance:** Grand entrance, precise heroic posing, then increasingly flimsy explanations once observed.
- **Visual continuity:** Striking but improvised superhero costume—recognisable silhouette, cheap or contradictory detail visible on closer inspection.

## Royal Guard
- **Story function:** Physical instrument of accidental reversals.
- **Episode 1 action:** Bursts through the main doors and unknowingly pushes the Daughter back into the position she is escaping.
- **Performance:** Absolute procedural seriousness; no awareness of the comic damage he creates.

## Master of Protocol / Herald
- **Story function:** Treats absurd etiquette as sacred law, escalating every misunderstanding.
- **Episode 1 action:** Measures and formalises the ceremonial handshake; may name the silence after the sound as a procedural crisis.
- **Performance:** Calm, reverent, exquisitely unhelpful.



### The King's Chair — Production Notes

# The King's Chair — Development Workflow

## Stage 1 — Series plot outline (current)
Build the season one episode at a time with Arek.

For each episode, Director supplies a short description in plain language:
- What happens
- The episode’s escalation / misunderstanding
- The Daughter’s escape attempt or reversal
- The King-Father’s inability to intervene
- A hook, twist, and cliffhanger only once the plot beat is agreed

Arek then either:
- Adds or corrects story events
- Chooses what happens next
- Asks Director to propose the next episode

Only then move to the following episode. The canonical record is `series-outline.md`.

## Parallel asset-development notes
As story decisions emerge, keep separate production profiles current. Do not bury asset requirements inside episode prose.

- `CHARACTERS.md` — identity, silhouette, wardrobe, performance, relationship, continuity locks
- `SETS.md` — locations, architecture, lighting, vertical framing opportunities, recurring set elements
- `PROPS.md` — story function, look, ownership, interaction, continuity locks

## Later stages
1. Lock the completed series outline.
2. Write each episode’s 1–2 minute script.
3. Make a scene breakdown.
4. Make a shot breakdown.
5. Create assets and generate shots.

## Rule
No full episode script, scene breakdown, or shot breakdown until the relevant series-outline episode is agreed.



### The King's Chair — Prop Bible

# Prop Profiles

## The King's Chair / Throne
- **Story function:** The father’s formal seat of power and private trap.
- **Required look:** Imposing, richly carved, overly official; it must make the father’s inability to stand visually painful.
- **Continuity lock:** Same throne geometry, armrests, upholstery, and position in every summit episode.

## Ceremonial Handshake Measure
- **Story function:** Establishes the court’s absurd reverence for protocol before the fart breaks it.
- **Required look:** A tiny ornate ruler or official measuring instrument handled by the Master of Protocol.
- **Episode 1 use:** It measures the dignity/precision of the peace handshake.

## Fake Superhero Costume
- **Story function:** Sells the Daughter’s rescue fantasy, then exposes the hero as a phony under scrutiny.
- **Required look:** Strong vertical silhouette at entrance; on closer framing reveal improvised/cheap construction, mismatched emblems, and suspiciously ordinary fastenings.
- **Continuity lock:** The same distinctive cape, mask, and emblem must survive across the reveal sequence.

## Kingdom Banners
- **Story function:** Make the rival courts instantly readable in a vertical frame.
- **Required look:** Two bold, visually distinct heraldic designs; avoid real-world national signifiers.
- **Continuity lock:** Each king, guard, and court zone aligns with the same banner language.



### The King's Chair — Set Bible

# Set Profiles

## The Peace Summit Hall
- **Story function:** One contained arena where a trivial bodily failure becomes a constitutional emergency.
- **Layout:** King-Father’s throne raised at centre; rival kings positioned opposite/below him; a wide public aisle; monumental main ceremonial doors at the far end; and an unobtrusive side door near the Daughter’s position.
- **Tone:** Excessively lavish, ancient, and serious—everything is built to make petty behaviour look historically momentous.
- **Vertical framing:** Throne height establishes the father’s formal authority but traps him; opposing king close-ups and the Daughter’s attempts to cross the lower frame create pressure.
- **Continuity locks:** Consistent throne position, banners for each kingdom, main-door and side-door orientation, Daughter’s escape route, and court audience zones.

## The Side Door
- **Story function:** The Daughter’s first plausible escape route—and the entrance that betrays her.
- **Tone / look:** A modest service or guard-access door made absurdly small beside the monumental ceremony.
- **Episode 1 action:** The Daughter barely opens it before a summoned army detachment storms in through it, sweeping her back into the centre of the summit.
- **Continuity lock:** Position it so the soldiers’ movement naturally pushes her toward the rival kings.
