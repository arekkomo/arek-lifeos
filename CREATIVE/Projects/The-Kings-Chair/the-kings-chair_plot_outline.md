---# THE KING’S CHAIR — Season One Series Outline

## Series Engine
- A peace summit between two rival kings collapses when the King-Father is physically trapped on his throne by an embarrassing, increasingly dangerous secret—and every attempt to explain it produces a worse constitutional emergency.
- The court treats absurd etiquette as sacred law: a cough is attempted murder, a travel bag is a declaration of war, and an unsigned marriage decree must be obeyed before anyone is allowed to ask who wrote it.
- The Daughter is treated as evidence, leverage, and a bride by everyone except herself; her determinedly practical escape turns court paranoia into a military crisis.
- **Continuity rule:** If an episode script changes its hook, twist, or cliffhanger, update the matching episode below before scene or shot work proceeds.

## EPISODE 1 — “THE SOUND”

The two rival kings have finally agreed to make peace and complete a long, overly dignified ceremonial handshake before the entire court. The King-Father rises from his throne to announce the peace, but the room falls completely silent—and an unmistakable fart breaks the moment.

The King-Father freezes. He is ashamed, terrified the next fart may be worse, and too frightened of offending either king to say anything. But the two kings are so consumed by mutual suspicion that neither considers him responsible. King One believes King Two has publicly mocked him. King Two immediately assumes King One made the sound, intends to blame him for it, and is acting in bad faith.

Their diplomatic argument escalates around the invisible father. In the confusion, the Daughter tries to sneak out through an unobtrusive side door; she wants neither king and wants no role in their conflict. Just as she cracks it open, a full detachment of soldiers storms into the ceremonial chamber through that same door, having been summoned by one of the rival kings. The rushing soldiers sweep her back into the room and physically push her directly between the two kings.

- **Hook:** The peace handshake ends to huge applause; the King-Father rises to speak, the court falls silent, and the sound erupts from his throne.
- **Twist:** The rival kings do not suspect the father. Each interprets the sound as the other king’s deliberate insult and proof of bad faith.
- **Cliffhanger:** The Daughter’s escape door bursts open with soldiers. She is carried back by the rush and deposited between the two kings. King One points to her and declares: **“She was fleeing to him.”**

## EPISODE 2 — “THE CHOICE”

The Daughter has been pushed between the two rival kings, and both treat her failed escape as proof that she must choose one of them. Their argument shifts from who insulted whom to who has the right to marry her. Every possible choice is terrible for her: choosing King One humiliates King Two; choosing King Two confirms King One’s suspicion; refusing either is treated as a political act of war.

The King-Father wants to intervene, but he is still too frightened of worsening the summit—and too physically trapped on his throne—to take control. Under unbearable pressure, the Daughter breaks and announces that she is already in love with someone else: a superhero.

A superhero arrives to great ceremonial commotion. For one brief second, the court and the audience share the Daughter’s relief: perhaps he really will rescue her. Then the attention lands on him. His confidence, costume, and heroic entrance are all a performance. He is not a superhero at all, but a phony who pretends to be one to impress women—and the Daughter has blindly fallen for it.

- **Hook:** The Daughter stands trapped between the kings as they demand an immediate answer: which one was she trying to flee to?
- **Twist:** Her declaration of love summons a supposed superhero—but his “heroism” collapses under scrutiny as a transparent romantic scam.
- **Cliffhanger:** The fake hero is exposed in front of the court. The Daughter’s only escape has made her look even more foolish and politically compromising, while the kings decide that the King-Father must now force a choice.

## EPISODE 3 — “THE FLY”

The King-Father finally attempts to take command. He gathers himself to stop the commotion, resolve the Daughter’s forced choice, and restore the summit’s dignity. Before he can speak, a fly flies directly into his mouth. He chokes violently, stumbles, and falls.

The two kings do not see an accident. They see an assassination attempt carried out during a contested royal marriage negotiation. Each immediately blames the other for sending the insect. The court treats the fly as evidence; the King-Father is unable to correct anyone because he is still choking.

- **Hook:** The King-Father finally rises to seize control of the room—then a fly flies into his open mouth.
- **Twist:** His choking is instantly reclassified as an attempted royal assassination, with the fly treated as a weapon.
- **Cliffhanger:** The two kings accuse each other of assassinating the father and reach for their guards, while the fallen King-Father can only choke and watch the room tilt toward war.

## EPISODE 4 — “THE DETENTION”
- **Progression:** Both courts claim the right to detain the Daughter, each politely pulling one arm while debating correct posture. A torn tapestry exposes a ceremonial portrait and a hidden passage. The portrait is of the King-Father in precisely his current trapped pose, painted years earlier with disturbing confidence.
- **Hook:** The King-Father nearly rises when the Daughter calls to him, then retreats into silence as the portrait appears to clear its throat.
- **Twist:** The portrait conceals a route out of the palace—and proves the court has been commemorating this exact humiliation for generations.
- **Cliffhanger:** Every person in court sees the hidden passage at once, then argues over whose protocol permits entering it first.

## EPISODE 5 — “THE HIDDEN PASSAGE”
- **Progression:** A guard searches the passage and finds a sealed proclamation. It is a marriage proclamation signed that morning by the King-Father, but it names no groom—and includes a menu for the wedding breakfast.
- **Hook:** The Daughter stands one step from the tunnel while each king claims it belongs to the other, and the tunnel’s custodian asks both to remove their shoes.
- **Twist:** The proclamation appears to prove the King-Father has already chosen the Daughter’s political future, though he has apparently chosen the pastries more decisively.
- **Cliffhanger:** The Daughter is dragged back from the passage as King One declares the father has chosen, and demands the custodian preserve the tunnel as a crime scene.

## EPISODE 6 — “THE PROCLAMATION”
- **Progression:** The kings demand the groom’s name. The Master of Protocol invokes ancient law: an unnamed royal proclamation requires an immediate public choice by the Daughter, followed by a toast, a hymn, and a mandatory donkey.
- **Hook:** The King-Father forces himself halfway out of the throne, trying to disown the proclamation, as a wedding donkey is led in far too early.
- **Twist:** The Daughter reveals the King-Father’s missing signet ring in her hand; the donkey reacts as if it recognizes it.
- **Cliffhanger:** The King-Father collapses back into the chair as the ring turns suspicion toward the Daughter and the donkey brays for silence.

## EPISODE 7 — “THE CHOICE”
- **Progression:** The Daughter says someone planted the ring on her during the struggle. A royal guard kneels and claims he acted on whispered orders from the throne, although the throne has only issued insults about his moustache.
- **Hook:** Both kings accuse the Daughter of forging the pass and proclamation, then demand she forge a second copy for comparison.
- **Twist:** The guard directly implicates the King-Father—and produces a signed receipt for the whisper.
- **Cliffhanger:** The King-Father tries to stand and deny it, but a second, more dangerous sound begins—and he sits instead as the court calls for the royal artillery.

## EPISODE 8 — “THE WEDDING”
- **Progression:** The rival kings finally agree that the King-Father orchestrated the deception and order an immediate marriage before anyone can change their mind. The Daughter escapes through the passage as the King-Father finally rises—and the wedding donkey follows her.
- **Hook:** Extreme close focus on the King-Father’s hands crushing the throne arms while the court awaits his denial and the herald rehearses the marriage announcement in three languages.
- **Twist:** The herald announces that the bride has chosen, but the Daughter is already gone; only the donkey remains to accept congratulations.
- **Cliffhanger:** An army arrives under the King-Father’s own royal seal; he is standing at last, but too late—and nobody can decide whether to salute the army or arrest it.

# The King's Chair — Season 1 Episode Engine

## Series promise
**A fragile royal peace summit collapses because its host cannot confess to a fart, cannot risk standing for a potentially worse second one, and cannot bring himself to protect his daughter from two men who regard her as a treaty.**

## Episode grammar
Each episode begins in the immediate fallout from the last ending. Within 1–2 minutes it must deliver:

- **Hook (0–3 sec):** the most alarming image, accusation, or impossible new fact.
- **Pressure:** the father attempts to remain neutral and physically contained; the daughter finds an exit.
- **Turn:** an ordinary action is misread as allegiance, insult, or betrayal.
- **Cliffhanger:** a new fact or public declaration changes the stakes, not merely delays the scene.

---

## Episode 1 — *The Sound*
**Hook:** A roaring court applauds as the two rival kings shake hands beneath the King-Father’s gaze: peace appears secured.

**Pressure:** He rises to make the announcement. Silence. A small, unmistakable sound escapes. He freezes in terror—not only from shame, but because he suspects the next one could be wet.

**Turn:** King One hears a public mockery. King Two instantly believes King One made the sound and intends to frame him. They begin accusing each other while ignoring the father completely.

**Cliffhanger:** The daughter slips toward the doors. A guard storms in, summoned by his king, and pushes her back between both rulers. King One points at her: **“She was fleeing to him.”**

## Episode 2 — *The Exit*
**Hook:** The daughter is held between the rival kings, each claiming the other tried to steal her.

**Pressure:** The father can end this with one sentence, but fears that speaking—or standing—will expose him. The daughter insists she was leaving *both* of them, which each king hears as a tactical lie.

**Turn:** Her abandoned travel bag is brought in from the service door. It contains a travel pass bearing King One’s crest. She has never seen it.

**Cliffhanger:** King Two opens the pass and finds it stamped with the **King-Father’s private royal seal.** The father sees it and knows it is genuine.

## Episode 3 — *The Seal*
**Hook:** King Two holds up the father’s seal: proof, apparently, that the father secretly arranged the daughter’s escape with King One.

**Pressure:** The father searches for an explanation without moving. A courtier reminds everyone that the seal turns a travel pass into sovereign instruction. The daughter tries to reach a side staircase while all eyes follow the document.

**Turn:** King One does not celebrate. He takes the pass, reads it, and realises it sends the daughter not to his territory—but through a border checkpoint controlled by King Two.

**Cliffhanger:** King One looks at King Two: **“You forged this to make me take the blame.”** King Two calls for the daughter to be detained as a conspirator.

## Episode 4 — *The Detention*
**Hook:** Guards seize the daughter as King Two orders her detained “for her own safety.”

**Pressure:** The father nearly rises to stop them. He feels the second fart threaten and sits back down; his failure to intervene is read as consent. The daughter uses the movement of the guards to edge toward a tapestry-covered servants’ corridor.

**Turn:** King One demands equal custody. Their guards pull the daughter in opposite directions, turning a custody dispute into a near-abduction in full court.

**Cliffhanger:** The tapestry tears down, revealing a **large ceremonial portrait of the father seated on the exact same throne**—and behind it, the forgotten hidden passage the daughter was trying to use. Every armed guard turns toward the opening.

## Episode 5 — *The Hidden Passage*
**Hook:** The daughter is one step from the hidden passage, and both kings believe it leads to the other’s secret plot.

**Pressure:** The father knows the passage leads outside the palace—his daughter could finally escape—but saying so would expose that he has allowed an unguarded route through the summit. He remains silent.

**Turn:** A guard searches the passage and finds not the daughter’s accomplice but a sealed **marriage proclamation**, prepared in advance for the peace ceremony. It names no groom.

**Cliffhanger:** The proclamation bears the father’s signature, dated that morning. King One says, **“Then the father has already chosen.”** The father has no idea when he signed it.

## Episode 6 — *The Proclamation*
**Hook:** Both kings demand that the father name the groom from his own signed proclamation.

**Pressure:** He is forced to lift himself from the chair for the first time. The daughter reaches the outer door amid the attention on him. He opens his mouth—not to defend her, but to buy time.

**Turn:** Before he can speak, the court’s Master of Protocol announces an ancient rule: if a royal proclamation names no groom, the daughter must choose publicly, immediately, in front of both courts.

**Cliffhanger:** The daughter steps forward to choose neither man—then her hand is pulled open. Inside is the King-Father’s missing signet ring.

## Episode 7 — *The Choice*
**Hook:** The daughter holds the missing signet. Both kings now believe she forged the passes and proclamation herself.

**Pressure:** She says the ring was pressed into her hand while she was being shoved through the crowd. The father knows this is possible, but cannot accuse a guard without choosing a side. He nearly stands to name the real culprit.

**Turn:** The guard who returned her in Episode 1 kneels and declares he acted on a whispered royal order. He points—not at either king—but at the father.

**Cliffhanger:** The court falls silent. The father is about to finally rise and deny it. From under his chair comes the beginning of a second, much more dangerous sound.

## Episode 8 — *The Wedding*
**Hook:** Extreme close framing: the father grips the throne, fighting the second sound while every person waits for his denial.

**Pressure:** He cannot stand, cannot speak cleanly, and cannot reveal that he did not give the order. His silence is interpreted as admission. The daughter uses the frozen court to edge toward the open passage again.

**Turn:** In a perverse act of unity, both rival kings agree the father manipulated them. To punish his duplicity and “restore honour,” they declare the marriage must happen at once—before either can be tricked again.

**Season cliffhanger:** The father finally begins to rise to protect his daughter. A herald’s voice interrupts: **“Your Majesties—the bride has chosen.”** Everyone turns. The daughter is gone; the empty passage door swings in the background. Then a distant royal horn sounds outside the palace: a third army has arrived, and its banner carries the King-Father’s own seal.

**Cut to black.**

## Directional guardrails
- The father’s fart is never visually explicit. Direction lives in his face, hands, rigid posture, the chair, and the terrified pause before he speaks.
- The daughter is not a chess-master. She is repeatedly mistaken for one because every simple escape action acquires political meaning.
- Each cliffhanger must be playable as a clean final 1–3 second image or line for a vertical feed.
- Do not resolve the father’s paralysis in Season 1. The story sells the next episode by putting action within reach, then interrupting it.
