# The King's Chair — Season 1 Episode Engine

## Series promise
**A fragile royal peace summit collapses because its host cannot confess to a fart, cannot risk standing for a potentially worse second one, and cannot bring himself to protect his daughter from two men who regard her as a treaty.**

## Episode grammar
Each episode begins in the immediate fallout from the last ending. Within 1–2 minutes it must deliver:

- **Hook (0–3 sec):** the most alarming image, accusation, or impossible new fact.
- **Pressure:** the father attempts to remain neutral and physically contained; the daughter finds an exit.
- **Turn:** an ordinary action is misread as allegiance, insult, or betrayal.
- **Cliffhanger:** a new fact or public declaration changes the stakes, not merely delays the scene.

---

## Episode 1 — *The Sound*
**Hook:** A roaring court applauds as the two rival kings shake hands beneath the King-Father’s gaze: peace appears secured.

**Pressure:** He rises to make the announcement. Silence. A small, unmistakable sound escapes. He freezes in terror—not only from shame, but because he suspects the next one could be wet.

**Turn:** King One hears a public mockery. King Two instantly believes King One made the sound and intends to frame him. They begin accusing each other while ignoring the father completely.

**Cliffhanger:** The daughter slips toward the doors. A guard storms in, summoned by his king, and pushes her back between both rulers. King One points at her: **“She was fleeing to him.”**

## Episode 2 — *The Exit*
**Hook:** The daughter is held between the rival kings, each claiming the other tried to steal her.

**Pressure:** The father can end this with one sentence, but fears that speaking—or standing—will expose him. The daughter insists she was leaving *both* of them, which each king hears as a tactical lie.

**Turn:** Her abandoned travel bag is brought in from the service door. It contains a travel pass bearing King One’s crest. She has never seen it.

**Cliffhanger:** King Two opens the pass and finds it stamped with the **King-Father’s private royal seal.** The father sees it and knows it is genuine.

## Episode 3 — *The Seal*
**Hook:** King Two holds up the father’s seal: proof, apparently, that the father secretly arranged the daughter’s escape with King One.

**Pressure:** The father searches for an explanation without moving. A courtier reminds everyone that the seal turns a travel pass into sovereign instruction. The daughter tries to reach a side staircase while all eyes follow the document.

**Turn:** King One does not celebrate. He takes the pass, reads it, and realises it sends the daughter not to his territory—but through a border checkpoint controlled by King Two.

**Cliffhanger:** King One looks at King Two: **“You forged this to make me take the blame.”** King Two calls for the daughter to be detained as a conspirator.

## Episode 4 — *The Detention*
**Hook:** Guards seize the daughter as King Two orders her detained “for her own safety.”

**Pressure:** The father nearly rises to stop them. He feels the second fart threaten and sits back down; his failure to intervene is read as consent. The daughter uses the movement of the guards to edge toward a tapestry-covered servants’ corridor.

**Turn:** King One demands equal custody. Their guards pull the daughter in opposite directions, turning a custody dispute into a near-abduction in full court.

**Cliffhanger:** The tapestry tears down, revealing a **large ceremonial portrait of the father seated on the exact same throne**—and behind it, the forgotten hidden passage the daughter was trying to use. Every armed guard turns toward the opening.

## Episode 5 — *The Hidden Passage*
**Hook:** The daughter is one step from the hidden passage, and both kings believe it leads to the other’s secret plot.

**Pressure:** The father knows the passage leads outside the palace—his daughter could finally escape—but saying so would expose that he has allowed an unguarded route through the summit. He remains silent.

**Turn:** A guard searches the passage and finds not the daughter’s accomplice but a sealed **marriage proclamation**, prepared in advance for the peace ceremony. It names no groom.

**Cliffhanger:** The proclamation bears the father’s signature, dated that morning. King One says, **“Then the father has already chosen.”** The father has no idea when he signed it.

## Episode 6 — *The Proclamation*
**Hook:** Both kings demand that the father name the groom from his own signed proclamation.

**Pressure:** He is forced to lift himself from the chair for the first time. The daughter reaches the outer door amid the attention on him. He opens his mouth—not to defend her, but to buy time.

**Turn:** Before he can speak, the court’s Master of Protocol announces an ancient rule: if a royal proclamation names no groom, the daughter must choose publicly, immediately, in front of both courts.

**Cliffhanger:** The daughter steps forward to choose neither man—then her hand is pulled open. Inside is the King-Father’s missing signet ring.

## Episode 7 — *The Choice*
**Hook:** The daughter holds the missing signet. Both kings now believe she forged the passes and proclamation herself.

**Pressure:** She says the ring was pressed into her hand while she was being shoved through the crowd. The father knows this is possible, but cannot accuse a guard without choosing a side. He nearly stands to name the real culprit.

**Turn:** The guard who returned her in Episode 1 kneels and declares he acted on a whispered royal order. He points—not at either king—but at the father.

**Cliffhanger:** The court falls silent. The father is about to finally rise and deny it. From under his chair comes the beginning of a second, much more dangerous sound.

## Episode 8 — *The Wedding*
**Hook:** Extreme close framing: the father grips the throne, fighting the second sound while every person waits for his denial.

**Pressure:** He cannot stand, cannot speak cleanly, and cannot reveal that he did not give the order. His silence is interpreted as admission. The daughter uses the frozen court to edge toward the open passage again.

**Turn:** In a perverse act of unity, both rival kings agree the father manipulated them. To punish his duplicity and “restore honour,” they declare the marriage must happen at once—before either can be tricked again.

**Season cliffhanger:** The father finally begins to rise to protect his daughter. A herald’s voice interrupts: **“Your Majesties—the bride has chosen.”** Everyone turns. The daughter is gone; the empty passage door swings in the background. Then a distant royal horn sounds outside the palace: a third army has arrived, and its banner carries the King-Father’s own seal.

**Cut to black.**

## Directional guardrails
- The father’s fart is never visually explicit. Direction lives in his face, hands, rigid posture, the chair, and the terrified pause before he speaks.
- The daughter is not a chess-master. She is repeatedly mistaken for one because every simple escape action acquires political meaning.
- Each cliffhanger must be playable as a clean final 1–3 second image or line for a vertical feed.
- Do not resolve the father’s paralysis in Season 1. The story sells the next episode by putting action within reach, then interrupting it.
