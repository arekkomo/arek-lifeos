# Date

**Working title.**

## Premise capture
At a cabin getaway, Adam arrives visibly battered—scratches, a conspicuous hickey, and the look of someone who has narrowly escaped something. Around the fire, he recounts a catastrophic blind date: every attempt to leave only binds him tighter to an intensely unsettling man and his increasingly strange domestic world. The story appears safely behind him—until Adam wakes in the shared twin room and discovers his date has arrived as the promised late guest.

## Opening direction
The flashback opens *before the date properly begins*: Adam is in a bar catching up with the friend who arranged the blind date. Mr D8 is late. Once the first round is done and the second is being ordered, Adam slips to the club toilet.

There, Adam has an uninvited first encounter with Mr D8. Mr D8 does **not** know Adam is his blind date; he is simply and clearly attracted to him. The exchange is weird and faintly creepy—funny enough to make Adam uneasy, but not enough for either man to connect it to the pending date. At the table, the recognition turns that toilet encounter into the true entrance and makes the blind date feel inescapable from its first beat.

**Rule:** Mr D8 is total confidence and sincere good intention. The comedy comes from the gap between his self-image and the bleak, inconvenient reality he brings into the room.

## Comic engine
- Dry, uncomfortable escalation with occasional physical comedy.
- Adam has reasonable instincts; his increasingly absurd predicament makes him look unreasonable.
- The flashback injuries become visual clues and payoff.
- The final reveal turns a contained bad-date story into a nightmare that has followed him to the cabin.

## Current questions
- **Creative decision:** the date genuinely believes the night is going brilliantly. His warmth and certainty—not conscious malice—make Adam's escape attempts look inexplicably rude.
- What is the central escalating trap that makes Adam unable to leave?
- What specific two-bed nighttime ritual creates the final bedroom set piece?
