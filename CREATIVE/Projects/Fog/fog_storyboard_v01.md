# Fog — Scene Progression V1

## Narrative Device
Aiah walks through the city as though it has become a museum of suspended human moments. She does not enter each scene or resolve it; she passes close enough for the viewer to read the fragment. The scenes are not random misery. They form a gradual argument: everyday coldness becomes exclusion, then threat, then the recognition that people have learned to look away.

Three background threads recur as Aiah moves forward, so the city gains memory:
- **The runner:** a young woman moving fast with a suitcase and phone, always one moment ahead of panic.
- **The father and child:** a father walking away while a small child reaches after him; later the child is with another adult, watching the departure become real.
- **The ignored person:** someone who first drops belongings in public, later sits unseen at the edge of a crowd, and finally is framed beneath an argument nobody interrupts.

Aiah never sees the full stories at once. The audience pieces them together as she does.

## SCENE S01 — The City Held Breathes
**Time:** 00:00–00:17 · Verse 1, opening lines

Aiah emerges into a bright-gray, fogged street and begins walking. The first frozen people are ordinary: commuters mid-step, a cyclist leaning into a turn, a café worker holding a cup just before it spills. The pause initially feels quiet and almost beautiful. The runner appears in the deep background, frozen beside a wheeled suitcase, staring at a phone.

**Purpose:** Establish the rule: Aiah moves through a city that feels paused, but the city is populated and real.

## SCENE S02 — Things Left Behind
**Time:** 00:17–00:33 · Verse 1, closing lines

Aiah passes a residential entrance. A father is caught walking away with a bag over his shoulder; a child’s hand is extended toward him. We do not show an impact or a melodramatic goodbye—only the moment before he turns. At the curb, the ignored person has dropped papers and groceries; everyone around them is frozen mid-stride, walking past.

**Purpose:** Seed abandonment and indifference beneath the city’s normal surface.

## SCENE S03 — The Crowd Narrows
**Time:** 00:33–00:42 · Pre-Chorus 1

Aiah enters a compressed pedestrian passage. The frozen bodies now make the route physically difficult: an argument held face-to-face, someone pushing through a turnstile without looking, hands about to collide around a doorway. Aiah must thread through them, never touching anyone. Fog turns the passage into a tunnel.

**Purpose:** Let the city begin to press on her before the first chorus releases the space.

## SCENE S04 — Public, Alone
**Time:** 00:42–01:10 · Chorus 1

The street opens onto a busy public square. Aiah performs the chorus in a slow frontal track. All around her, people are frozen in dense social arrangements: laughing groups whose bodies close a circle against someone standing just outside it; a person talking into a phone while the runner is visible behind them, still trapped in a private emergency; the ignored person now seated at the square’s edge, unnoticed.

**Purpose:** State the thesis visually: the world is crowded, moving in implication, and emotionally inaccessible.

## SCENE S05 — Time Has Weight
**Time:** 01:11–01:29 · Verse 2

Aiah continues past a transit stop. The runner is now mid-call, suitcase tipped on its side, while the train doors are frozen almost shut behind her. Across the street, the father is further away; the child is now held by another adult but still faces the direction he left. The fog has thickened enough to erase the end of the block.

**Purpose:** Turn the earlier fragments into a felt backstory without explaining any of them.

## SCENE S06 — The Boundary
**Time:** 01:29–01:37 · Pre-Chorus 2

Aiah passes the most confrontational image: an older teenager or adult has one hand raised in anger toward the ignored person, who is backed against a shop window. A nearby group remains frozen in the act of looking away. On “You ain’t gonna tell me shit today,” Aiah walks past without turning her head.

**Purpose:** Her boundary is not indifference; it is self-protection after an accumulation she cannot carry.

## SCENE S07 — The World She Refuses
**Time:** 01:38–02:06 · Chorus 2

The camera widens as Aiah crosses another square. Background threads now coexist in the same city field: the runner, father/child, and ignored person are each held at different distances. The repetition makes the city feel patterned rather than accidentally cruel. Aiah sings directly to camera once, then looks past it toward the fog.

**Purpose:** Make the chorus a decision: she refuses participation in this version of the world.

## SCENE S08 — The Cat
**Time:** 02:13–02:28 · Bridge

The noise and density fall away beneath an awning, at a platform edge, or beside a shuttered storefront. Aiah notices a cat. The cat is the only other creature that moves. They hold each other’s gaze; Aiah lowers herself and almost reaches toward it. For several seconds, the paused city is no longer the whole world.

The cat turns and walks into the fog in one direction. Aiah watches, then stands and walks in the other.

**Purpose:** Give Aiah a genuine opening toward connection without changing the song’s ending into resolution.

## SCENE S09 — Fog Takes the Frame
**Time:** 02:30–03:11 · Final Chorus and Outro

Aiah returns to the street. The city’s suspended scenes are now barely visible, reduced to pale figures behind moving fog. She walks toward camera, then past it, slowly dissolving into the whiteness. On “and that’s okay,” hold on the street after she has gone. One tiny visual shift—a hand lowering, a train door completing its close, or the cat crossing deep in frame—can imply that time may be returning, but never confirm it.

**Purpose:** End on withdrawal as a temporary shelter, not a victory or a death.

## Continuity Rules
- The three recurring threads must remain background discoveries, never cutaway subplots.
- Hold human action precisely still; allow fog, light rain, vapor, loose paper, and the cat to move subtly.
- The progression is from *strange stillness* → *social indifference* → *threat* → *one fragile connection* → *chosen disappearance*.
- Do not show explicit injury, physical impact, or an explanation of any backstory. The viewer supplies the missing seconds.
