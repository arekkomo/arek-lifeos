# Fog

## Concept

A woman announces with full diva energy that she's checking out of the world today — phone off, door locked. But as the song unfolds, the bravado becomes transparent. The sass is real, but so is the bruise underneath: a lost love that still lives in the apartment, in the bedsheets, in the fog outside the window. The song mashes two sonic personalities — hip-hop diva attitude in the pre-choruses vs. Cigarettes After Sex-style dreamy intimacy in the verses and chorus — and lets them coexist. The fog is both shelter and shroud.

## Core Duality

Armor ↔ Tenderness — the eye-roll and the whisper are the same act of self-preservation.

## Central Metaphor

Fog as the world disappearing so she doesn't have to deal. Not grief — retreat. The fog isn't something to endure; it's something that arrived for her.

## Performance Notes

- Verse 1: Lazy, half-sung, lots of space between lines. Breath-close delivery. The sensory details (smell, warmth, touch) should feel physical.

- Pre-Chorus 1: Slight melodic lift, widening harmonies start to come in. Still intimate but opening up.

- Chorus: Bigger, melodic, catchy. Reverb-heavy. This is where the 80s ad-libs live.

- Verse 2: Even lazier than V1. Time bending — the lyrics are about lost hours, so the delivery should feel slowed, drugged.

- Pre-Chorus 2: The diva snaps back. "You ain't gonna tell me shit today" — this is the armor line. Sass returns hard.

- Bridge: Drums drop, vocal close and bare. Whispered. The confession moment.

- Final Chorus: Full lift. Ad-libs, backing vocals, all the production layered in. The "and that's okay" is the emotional release.

## Suno Settings

- Weirdness: low-mid (~30%)

- Style Influence: high (~75%) to keep the genre blend coherent

- Expect 3-5 takes to get the sass/softness balance right

- Use Replace Section if ad-libs leak into verse openings
