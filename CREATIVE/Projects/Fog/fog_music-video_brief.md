# Fog — Music Video

## Core Concept
Aiah walks alone through a city that is alive but feels inhospitable, unpleasant, and emotionally unsafe. For her, the world is on pause: people and events are frozen in the midst of ordinary life and gradually reveal moments of sorrow, cruelty, pressure, and indifference. She moves reluctantly through it without interacting.

The song’s “I ain't in the mood for the world today” is the visual premise. The video does not claim Aiah is the only living thing, nor that the world has objectively stopped. The freeze is her subjective withdrawal—a protective pause that lets her pass through a world she cannot face.

## Emotional Arc
- **Opening:** The city is quietly uncanny; Aiah moves while ordinary moments remain suspended.
- **Development:** Each frozen scene exposes more discomfort beneath daily life.
- **Revelation:** By the later choruses, the accumulation explains her retreat without a literal explanatory scene.
- **Ending:** Aiah continues into the fog, choosing distance over engagement. Whether the city resumes is left unresolved.

## Directing Rules
- Aiah is calm, inward, and reluctant—not triumphant, magical, or emotionally numb.
- The city must feel populated and active in implication; frozen people should be caught mid-action, never posed as decorative tableaux.
- The video reveals harm progressively and avoids exploitation or generic catastrophe imagery.
- The fog is both visual atmosphere and Aiah’s emotional boundary.

## Visual Language
Bright, overcast, cold gray daylight. Fog softens a busy city into pale concrete, wet asphalt, dim glass, and distant figures. The result is depressing but beautiful: loneliness exists even in crowded spaces. As Aiah walks, the frozen human interactions behind her progressively feel hostile and unpleasant.
