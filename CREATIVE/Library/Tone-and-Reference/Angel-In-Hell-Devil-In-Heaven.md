# Angel In Hell, Devil In Heaven

A lyrical world for the feeling of being wrong for every room, without naming that feeling directly. The central contrast is not moral judgment; it is permanent miscasting—an angel who cannot survive Hell and a devil who cannot rest in Heaven.

Use objects that almost fit as emotional evidence: every glove too tight, a bed too bright, borrowed halos slipping sideways, smoke caught in stained-glass light. Let the images hold the drama rather than explaining it.

The musical form should be sweeping melodic pop: wide choruses that promise release, while the lyrics leave the speaker without a safe place to land. Use rhyme families and vowel flow to make the language feel inevitable rather than literary-for-its-own-sake.

Useful for songs or scenes about exile, self-doubt, social misfit, or being caught between opposing identities.
