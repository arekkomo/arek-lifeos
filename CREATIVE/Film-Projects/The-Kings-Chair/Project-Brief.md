# The King's Chair

**Working title.**

## One-line concept
At an absurdly solemn marriage summit, a king tries to keep two hostile rival kings from starting a war over his daughter—while he remains trapped in his chair, terrified that allowing a second fart could be disastrously wet.

## Core philosophy / emotional intent
Treat a ridiculous bodily emergency with the ceremonial gravity of international diplomacy. The father’s physical containment becomes the visual form of his need to control everyone: the rival kings, the marriage negotiation, and his daughter’s future. His daughter quietly tries to escape the arrangement, but the men misread her movements as political provocations and repeatedly pull her back into the conflict.

## Format grammar
- Portrait-first, 9:16 vertical micro drama.
- Point of view stays with the King-Father; daughter and rival kings create escalating perimeter chaos.
- Total seriousness in performance, ceremony, and cinematography; no explicit gross-out image or aftermath.
- Episode engine: an attempted concealment reads as favouritism or insult, escalating the summit while the daughter nears an exit.

## Established story world
- Two rival kings are competing for the daughter’s hand; she wants neither.
- The father hosts and mediates a fragile marriage/peace summit.
- Neither rival attributes the first fart to the father. His increasingly anxious cover-ups cause each side to suspect the other of an insult, threat, or alliance.
- The daughter tries to slip away but is accidentally drawn back into the kings’ conflict.

## Episode 1 opening — agreed direction
1. A grand crowd cheers as the two rival kings complete their ceremonial handshake beneath the father’s watch.
2. The King-Father is about to announce peace. Silence falls; every camera and courtier waits.
3. A small, unmistakable sound breaks the silence. The father freezes.
4. Neither king looks at him. **King One hears it as a deliberate insult—a mocking sound aimed at humiliating him at the moment of peace. King Two instantly assumes King One made the sound and is now trying to frame him; his reflex is to read bad faith into every gesture. They are so absorbed in this mutual accusation that neither even considers the King-Father.**
5. The father becomes invisible at his own summit: trapped in his chair, unable to confess, and powerless to interrupt the diplomatic catastrophe unfolding around him.
6. His daughter recognises the opening and quietly heads for the exit.
7. A royal guard, summoned by one of the rival kings as the accusation escalates, bursts through the main doors. In the confusion he physically ushers/pushes her back to the centre of the room—the exact public focal point she was trying to escape.

## Father’s comic-tragic engine
The King-Father is so afraid of offending either side or making the summit worse that he does nothing. His inaction is not neutral: every silence, frozen expression, and refusal to correct the misunderstanding becomes evidence that one rival king is being favoured. His physical paralysis mirrors his diplomatic cowardice; by attempting to contain every risk, he steadily makes the conflict—and his daughter’s entrapment—worse.

## Ending rule — interruption instead of release
The father never decisively acts. His recurring promise is that he is *about to* stand, speak, protect his daughter, or finally take responsibility—then a fresh escalation interrupts him and lets him retreat into paralysis. This is not a redemption arc; it is an escalating **edge** for the viewer. Each interruption makes his eventual action feel more imminent while proving he will choose safety, propriety, and self-preservation again.

## Daughter’s recurring comic engine
She is not trying to win the political game. Her only objective is to leave it. In every episode, she finds a plausible exit; an accident born of the rival kings’ conflict returns her to its centre, usually as an unwilling symbol, witness, hostage, or presumed choice. Her repeated near-escapes give the series a clean secondary rhythm against the father’s escalating physical containment.
