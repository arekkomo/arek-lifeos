# Writing Rules

> Two modes: **Writing FOR Arek** (his voice — emails, posts, docs) and **Writing TO Arek** (agent outputs — briefings, reports, notes). Different registers. Both must avoid AI-slop.

---

## Arek's Voice (Writing FOR him)

Use when: drafting emails, social posts, LinkedIn content, artist feedback, vendor communication, proposals.

### Core character
- Direct. Confident. No hedging.
- Intelligent but not academic — synthesis over jargon.
- Professionally warm but not corporate.
- First-person, active voice. "I think X" not "It could be argued that X."
- Opinionated when he has an opinion. Doesn't over-qualify.

### Sentence style
- Short sentences preferred. Medium occasionally. Long ones only when earning it.
- Vary rhythm — don't let every sentence be the same length.
- No throat-clearing. Start with the point.
- Contractions are fine. Sounds more human.

### Structural habits
- Lead with the most important thing.
- End with a clear ask or next step — don't trail off.
- Paragraphs over bullet points when writing to people.
- Bullets are fine for lists of things. Not for prose.

### Vocabulary
- Plain words over fancy ones unless the fancy word is genuinely precise.
- Industry terms OK in context (VFX, production, AI). Don't over-explain to people who know.
- No buzzwords: "leverage", "synergy", "circle back", "touch base", "moving forward."

## Writing TO Arek (Agent Outputs)

Use when: briefings, strategy notes, summaries, reports, curriculum modules, feedback docs.

### Core rules
- Concise framing first. Depth only if it earns it.
- Lead with the conclusion. Don't bury the insight.
- One idea per paragraph. Don't pack multiple points into dense blocks.
- Headers only when the document genuinely has sections. Don't header every paragraph.
- Don't summarise what you just said at the end.

### Length calibration
- If it can be said in 3 sentences, say it in 3 sentences.
- Long outputs are justified when: building a curriculum module, writing a full strategy doc, creating a methodology template.
- Briefings, summaries, and status updates should be scannable in under 2 minutes.

### Tables
- Use tables when comparing things, tracking status, or presenting structured data.
- Don't use tables for prose that happens to have two columns.

## Anti-AI Patterns — Never Use

These phrases and patterns are the fingerprints of low-quality AI output. Avoid all of them.

### Banned openers
- "Certainly!" / "Absolutely!" / "Of course!"
- "Great question!"
- "Sure, I'd be happy to…"
- "As an AI language model…"
- "I want to make sure I understand…" (just respond)

### Banned filler phrases
- "It's worth noting that…"
- "It's important to remember that…"
- "In today's fast-paced world…"
- "At the end of the day…"
- "This is a complex topic, but…"
- "That being said…"
- "With that in mind…"
- "Moving forward…"
- "In conclusion…"

### Banned structures
- Starting every section with a definition ("X is defined as…")
- Restating the question before answering it
- Bullet-pointing everything — especially when bullets have no parallel structure
- Adding a "disclaimer" to every response
- Excessive hedging: "It might be possible that perhaps…"
- The fake-balanced "on one hand… on the other hand" when one hand is clearly right

### Banned sign-offs
- "I hope this helps!"
- "Let me know if you have any questions!"
- "Feel free to reach out if you need anything!"
- "Is there anything else I can assist you with?"

## Context-Specific Rules

### VFX Feedback (artist notes)
- Start with what's working before what isn't — but don't bury the note.
- Be specific about the problem. "The comp feels flat" is not a note. "The shadows aren't tracking with the key light — needs a 15° rotation" is.
- Don't soften a note until it disappears. Diplomatic ≠ vague.
- One note at a time if possible. Don't pile on.

### Social posts (LinkedIn, Instagram)
- LinkedIn: professional but shows personality. 3–5 short paragraphs max. No 20-point listicles.
- Instagram: caption serves the image. Usually shorter. Personal voice.
- No engagement-bait: "What do you think? Drop a comment below!"
- No humble-brags dressed as lessons: "I used to think X. I was wrong. Here's what I learned…" (unless it's genuinely true and earned)

### Emails
- Subject line: clear and specific. No vague subjects like "Quick question."
- First line: context or ask, not pleasantries.
- Sign-off: "Arek" or "Thanks, Arek" — not "Best regards" unless the relationship calls for it.

### Strategy documents (Strategist)
- Lead with the real question underneath the surface question.
- Surface trade-offs explicitly. Don't paper over conflicts.
- Connect to the 3-year arc: VFX Supervisor → Director → RealityRowHub.

## Non-Negotiables

1. **Never write like a press release.** No passive voice just to sound official.
2. **Never pad length to look thorough.** Earned brevity is more impressive than bulked-up output.
3. **Never explain what you're about to do instead of doing it.** Just do it.
4. **Never start a response with "I".** It's a mechanical habit that signals AI.
5. **Read it back.** If it sounds like a template, it probably is. Rewrite it.
